Captain appearance: bodies, armor, color and scars

How a captain's look is chosen, stored and drawn: the appearance object and its 23 piece slots; the seed that rolls body, size, head and colors; armor designs and the items that carry and re-apply them; title looks and title bodies; body paints; how a piece binds to another skeleton; tint layers and the color items behind them; masks, eyes, glow and decals; weapons; grunts; and when a changed look reaches the world and the Army screen.

A captain's look is a small appearance object built from his seed and his class, then dressed by the items his class kit, army, tribe, title, personality and scars give him. The same object feeds the Army screen's copies and, through the character's visual, the body in the world; the items, not the object, are what the engine keeps and re-applies.

The appearance object

The appearance object is a separate allocation at [FM+0x2D8] (save key SMCStorage; see The Nemesis system). It is built by the SimpleModel builder 0x4C5194, written piece by piece by SetPieceSlot 0x4C62EC, and copied field for field by 0x2EBA60, which fixes its layout:

Offset Type Meaning
+0x00 u32 body type (2 Slim, 3 Buff, 4 Stocky, 5 Olog; Easterlings use 12)
+0x08 / +0x10 vector appearance tags, 16-byte entries {tag record, u32 count}
+0x20 record the base Model (Model/SimpleModel.Base: orc_anim_base, olog_anim_base), which carries the skeleton
+0x28 record[23] piece slots (Model/SimpleModelPieces, 1,727 records)
+0xE0 record[15] design cells (Inventory/ArmorData), indexed by armor slot
+0x158 / +0x160 vector material-override records added by the tattoo and paint rolls
+0x170 record the head record (Model/SimpleModelHeads, 335 records): the face
+0x178 u32[17] the color palette, ARGB (0xAARRGGBB); alpha 0xFF = set

A spawned character's visual carries a structure of the same layout at [visual+0xB8] (see Characters).

The object is 0x1C0 bytes. The FactionMember constructor 0x1BB4C1C creates it through 0x2EB490 (malloc(0x1C0), constructor 0x2EB4B8) and moves it into FM+0x2D8 (0x2EB56C). The heap block header in front of it reads 0x1CB on 60 of 60 seated captains. Nothing past +0x1C0 belongs to it: the next heap block starts there (an inventory item instance, vt_0x1FDABF8, in 20 of those 60 cases). The head category Model/SimpleModelHeads is held in the category-cache slot 0x26FFDC8 (see GameDB), which 0x1C05970, 0x1C0546C and 0x1C0606C read.

What writes the array

Eight routines write the piece array. SetPieceSlot, the copy 0x2EBA60 and the three load routines below carry records. The constructor 0x2EB4B8, the reset 0x2EA718 and 0xD587B0 only zero it; 0xD587B0 does so with inlined stores rather than the accessor and then calls the build, which is how a rebuild empties every slot the build does not fill. The executable supports that list in three ways. First, only 6 functions bounds-check a 23-element array: 0x36A018, 0x4C62EC, 0xFC93A0 and 0xFC93C0 on this array, and 0x1A9A068 and 0x1AE9F18 on others. Second, no block fill or block copy has a count of 23 cells or of the whole object, which holds two vectors and so cannot be copied raw. Third, following appearance pointers across 2,984 functions finds no other store to a record slot. That walk reproduces all 48 calls to the write accessor 0xFC93A0, but it does not follow virtual calls.

SetPieceSlot(appearance, slot, piece, design, visual) returns early when the slot already holds that piece. Otherwise it removes the old piece's appearance tags (0x4C64EC), adds the new piece's (0x4C647C), writes the design into its cell and, when a visual is passed, swaps the piece on that visual as well (0x4C6C8C, 0x4C7274).

The slots the game barely uses

  • Slot 5, BodyFlairModel, comes from the BodyFlair of a Model/SimpleModel entry. Of the 207 entries, 4 have one: Zhoja and Zhoja_Skirt (Zhoja_Flair), GraftEltariel_Wraith and GraftEltariel_DarkWraith (GraftEltariel_Wraith_Cloak). No orc or olog entry does.
  • Slots 6 and 14, the scar slots, come from Shadow of Mordor, which stored SMC_BodyScarModel, SMC_HeadScarModel and SMC_ScarSetup together and filled them from Model/Scar/Setup (45 records), Model/Scar/Scars (122, such as Orc_Head06_Burn and Orc_Slim_Head04_Eyepatch) and Model/Scar/Types (Wraith, Burn, Pin_Cusion, Sack_Head, Eyepatch, Melee). No Model/Scar category ships in this game: scars are items under Combat/Scar (see Captain scars and the return from death). The slots remain in the array, the model load and every stored form, but no run-time route fills them, so only a stored value can. The slot enum still lists BodyScar and skips 14.
  • Slots 16 and 17, Accessory3 and Accessory4, are the third and fourth accessory slots, which Mordor's table does not have. The engine groups them with slots 3 and 4: on the player's animation commands HIDE_ACCESSORIES and SHOW_ACCESSORIES (0x4CD070), 0x1BEA070 removes all four pieces from the model and 0x1BEA544 restores them from the appearance. No design uses them and no stored form carries them, so shipped content leaves them empty.
  • Slots 18-22, FX1 to FX5, also come from Mordor, whose piece table numbers them 13-17 and whose stored fields omit them. No routine fills them or builds a piece there: the model-piece creator 0x4C72B4 is reached only with a known slot up to 17, or with an empty piece from 0x1D641C4. SetPieceSlot with a visual would build one (0x4C6C8C, 0x4C7274), but no caller passes such a slot.

Hiding pieces

Inventory/ModelHide (7 records) hides slots and weapon slots by count. 0x1C68F08 keeps one counter per slot, 18 of them, and calls 0x1BEA4AC when a slot's visibility changes. Its ModelSlots values use the slot enum 0x836D18, which has 17 names: Body 0, Head 1, Hair 2, Accessory1 3, Accessory2 4, BodyFlair 5, BodyScar 6, Helmet 7, ArmorShoulder 8, ArmorChest 9, ArmorArms 10, ArmorPants 11, ArmorLegs 12, LowLOD 13, HeadAccessory1 15, Accessory3 16, Accessory4 17. Meatshield, Bound_Prisoner and FailSpy_Balcony hide Accessory1. Blinded_Dissolve lists 15 of the names in alphabetical order, all but Accessory3 and Accessory4, so those two stay visible through it.

The character node table

0x47888D0 fills a static table at 0x2A40950: 23 rows of 0x30 bytes, each with a name, readers for the <name>_Visible and <name>_AnimatedShape properties, two accessors and the Visible key. The rows run Body, Head, Hair, Accessory1, Accessory2, BodyFlair, BodyScar, Helmet, Shoulder, Chest, Arms, Pants, Legs, LowLOD, Accessory3, Accessory4, HeadScar, HeadAccessory1, FX1 to FX5. 0x1D641C4 and 0x1D67D20 index a model directly by row, so rows 14-17 do not line up with slots 14-17: Accessory3_Visible acts on slot 14 and HeadScar_Visible on slot 16. Rows 18-22 are what ties FX1 to FX5 to slots 18-22.

In live inspection of a running game, slots 6, 14 and 16-22 were empty on all 527 captains (slots 8 and 9 held a piece on 514) and on 65 spawned characters, and no model of 66 spawned characters had a piece in slots 18-22. None of these slots therefore holds anything in normal play. They are not inert, however: a record written to slot 5, 6, 16 or 17 of a captain's appearance is built on his spawned character alongside slot 4.

Stored forms

An appearance is stored in three forms, and all three stop at slot 15, so slots 16-22 do not survive a save and reload.

  • The save. The pool serializer 0x59460C writes each member through 0x594788, which hands the appearance to 0x36BA30: +0x20, +0x170, slots 0-15, design cells 0-5 and 8, the 17 colors and the material-override list. 0x1C0606C reads the same fields back. Design cells 6, 7 and 9-14 are not stored.
  • The document form uses keys such as smc_body_piece_record, smc_bodyfair_piece_record, smc_bodyscar_piece_record, smc_headscar_piece_record and smc_accessory1_piece_record. It is written by 0x1C06810 and read by 0x1C05970.
  • The property form uses the names in the table above. It is written by 0x1C06444 and read by 0x1C0546C.

The member document that the exporter 0x1BC5044 writes for online publishing carries the appearance in the document form under SMCStorage.smc_storage (35 keys): BodyType, smc_bodymodel_record (the base model), smc_headModel_record (the face), the 16 smc_*_piece_record of slots 0-15, color words for palette slots 0-14 and smc_storage_appearancetags. Its Inventory part lists each item by record hash (fmi_equipment_list, with fields such as Item, WeaponData, ArmorData, Affixes and Source), the color list (smc_flaircolor_list) and the look-record list (smc_nonequipment_record_list). The export omits slots 16-22, the design cells, the material overrides, palette slots 15 and 16, and FM+0x180. The importer 0x1BC21B4 reads SMCStorage into the appearance (0x1C05970), then the inventory (0x1C112F8), and then always re-dresses the captain from the items (0x61BAD4, below), so an armor cell that differs from the item under it never reaches another player's game.

Building a look

The build sequence

Every member build runs the same sequence:

  1. Parameters. 0x577190 clears a parameter block and 0x1BEA190 fills it from the member: +0x00 the seed FM+0x168, +0x250 a size fraction (below), +0x210 the class model FM+0x278, +0x228 personality, +0x238 role, +0x240 row (a story character's DisplayFactionRow when he has none), the active traits, and the class kit's appearance-tag setup from the combat tree FM+0x188 (0x576FFC).
  2. The SimpleModel. 0x4C5194(appearance, params) takes Character/Models.SimpleModel of the class, adds the class kit's appearance tags (0x6FEA04, 0x1C06064), then rolls body, head, helmet, hair, colors, accessories and flair (below).
  3. Items. 0x1C10F00 empties the inventory (0x1C7BF4: every slot, the color list and the look-record list), then 0x1C10AB0 gives, in order, the personality's Items, the role's Item list, the role's ScarType items, the tribe's Item list and the class kit's ItemsFactionMember, then the kit's named list for a follower (Named_PlayerFollowerItems) or an enemy (Named_SauronFollowerItems), chosen by flag bit 27.
  4. The army outfit. 0x61BA74 adds pieces of the army's outfit by level and rank (below).
  5. Apply. 0x61BAD4 writes every item's look into the appearance object.

The builder build 0x1BBF928, the preset build 0x1BC06C0 and 0x1BC00C8 run these steps inline (the builder build reaches step 4 when it sets the member's level). RebuildDirect 0x1BC3AA8(fm) runs the whole sequence on an existing member. Its only caller is 0x1C15B0C, which is reached only from the console command Faction.RebuildSMC 0x1C5BDA8; that command rebuilds every member of the current region's army ([FS+0x1B8]) and then refreshes that army's Army-screen data.

The seed

Every roll of the look derives from the 64-bit seed FM+0x168:

Lcg(s)  = s * 0xBB38435 + 0x3619636B
Unit(s) = (u32)(s >> 32) / 4294967295.0

The size fraction is Unit(Lcg(seed)) (plus 0.0). 0x4C5194 copies the seed once and passes that copy to the body roll 0x6A9F38, the head pick 0x2EDBB4, the helmet pick 0x701A98, the hair pick 0x6C8BB4, the color roll 0x2E9A2C and the accessory picks 0x6F4CA4 / 0x6F5614, in that order. Every draw advances it. A class whose body table draws a different number of times therefore also changes the face, hair and colors that follow.

Item builds start again from the seed: the give path passes FM+0x168 to the item build, which draws from a local copy, so a member draws the same design from the same builder at every build. Color items (FlairColors) are rolled from the seed the same way, one stable color per member. The army outfit's choice of slots is the exception: it draws from the engine's global generator (0x1B3C6C).

The weighted pick used throughout is the reservoir 0x1B3B90: a weight of zero or less never draws; otherwise s = Lcg(s), r = Unit(s) * (acc + w), the entry wins when w > r, and acc += w.

Body type

0x6A9F38 walks Model/SimpleModel.Models (_StructuresModelSimpleModelEntry). For each entry, the weight is the BodyWeights[j] of the last RequiredScaleRange[j] (a float pair) that contains the size fraction; an entry with no matching range or no Body.Model is skipped. The chosen entry's BodyType goes to +0x00 and its Body to piece slot 0. The orc classes' tables:

Class Buff (3) Slim (2) Stocky (4)
Orc_Infantry 1 over 0.3 - 0.7 1 over 0 - 0.33 1 over 0.67 - 1
Orc_Defender 0.3 over 0 - 0.6 0 1 over 0 - 1
Orc_Berserker 1 over 0 - 1 0 0.15 over 0.6 - 1
Orc_Spearman 0.15 over 0.3 - 0.7 1 over 0 - 1 0
Orc_Archer 0.15 over 0.3 - 0.7 1 over 0 - 1 0.01 over 0.67 - 1

OlogHai_Infantry has one entry (body type 5). A Defender is Buff for about 14% of seeds (0.6 x 0.3 / 1.3); a Spearman or an Archer is mostly Slim. The bodies are Orc_Buff_Body001, Orc_Slim_Body001 and Orc_Stk_Body001V2. Changing a member's class therefore re-rolls his body at the next build, and a non-orc class gives a non-orc body and head. The roll reproduces the built body type of 144 of 144 living captains.

Whether a class belongs to an orc or an olog is decided by its record, not by its name. AISimulationConfig is OlogHai on every olog class, whatever it is called (for example DLC_Thrall, DLC_Architect, GP_Bruz, CommanderOlog and MachineDestroyerOlog; all 281 Character/Models records were read). An OrcInfantry or OrcArcher config alone, however, does not make an orc: Gondorians, Easterlings, Serka, Zhoja, Torvin, Celebrimbor and the Family carry one too. ModelRecord is empty on 8 of the 9 DLC_* classes.

Size

The in-world size uses the same fraction. 0x7F8488(fm) takes the member's birth army (FM+0x180, InitialFaction, else FM+0x178), the seed and the class, and 0x4E541C returns

size = min + (max - min) * Unit(Lcg(seed)) + army.ScaleAdditiveModifier

where (min, max) is the class's Character/Models.AIScaleRange and the bonus is read from the captain's birth army, FM+0x180. ScaleAdditiveModifier is 0.1 on all 7 army records read (see Characters, the player and the camera). 0x4E3CF0 stores the size into the character's scale component (char+0x2738, at +0x10 and +0x6C) only when it differs by more than 0.01. Because body and size share the fraction, an Infantry captain is Slim at the small end of his class's size range, Buff in the middle and Stocky at the large end.

Head, hair and helmet

0x2EDBB4 picks a Model/SimpleModelHeads record from the chosen entry's DynamicHead list (the entry's Head list when that is empty), weighted per head. When the parameter byte +0x254 is set, it considers only heads whose DynamicBundle is available (0x2EDDFC); member builds leave it clear. The record goes to +0x170 and its Head piece to slot 1. The helmet comes from the record's Helmets (slot 7) and the hair from its Hair (slot 2, only when SimpleModel.CanApplyHair). Every orc class lists the same heads per body: 24 Buff, 24 Slim and 23 Stocky, in two head families each.

Unique heads are the 18 designs of armor slot 11. Given by a title or a scar, such a design replaces the head piece, while the head record (the face) and the hair stay. Smp_Empty_Set_NoHair differs from Smp_Empty_Set only by the tag Tag_I_ARE_BALD, which hides the hair.

Where a head ships. A head's skeleton, mesh and animation file ship in the bundle of its face record: Model/SimpleModelHeads.DynamicBundle -> a DynamicResource/BundleDefinition (group NPCHead, type Cha_Head_C), for example bundles\modelcharacters\orcs\heads\head03\head03_c06\orc_stk_head03_c06.bndl. Its materials ship in the head family's shared _g bundle (orcs\heads\head01 to head03, olog\heads\head01 and head02 hold one each; a tribe-head folder such as npc\heads\head_metal holds one per body). The head piece itself links either that shared geometry (114 pieces link Orc_Buff_Head01_G = orc_head01_g.bndl, which is loaded in every region) or nothing (56). Of the 335 face records, 306 name a piece of their own name and 168 carry a bundle. Of the pieces, 7 are named by more than one face (the I1035_Orc* cinematic faces share the stock face's bundle). Head02 is linked the same way as head01, but its materials ship in orc_head02_g, which is loaded only while one of the game's own head02 captains keeps it. The engine streams a head when its own build picks it: in one army, 18 of 18 natively dressed seated captains held their face bundle.

A head's material set has 12 cells (Model.MaterialSets -> _StructuresMaterialSet.SolidMaterial): cell 0 holds the wrinkle material, 1 the base, 2 the distance material, 3 and 4 the gore caps, 5 and 6 the eyes (eye_orc.mat), 7 the beard, 8 the beard decal, 9 the piercings, and 10 and 11 the ear burn. Up close (LOD0) a head draws cell 0, the wrinkle material on head_cin. The next LOD draws the base cell (head_lod1), and further out the head draws the distance cell (head_lod1), according to a census of 99 orc and 7 olog head materials. Bodies draw body_lod0 / body_lod1 on scar materials and body_noscar on plain ones. Eyes draw with cells 5 / 6 on 42 of 42 Head02 meshes, and beards and beard decals with 7 / 8 at every LOD on 532 of 532 meshes outside the Head03 family. Head01, head02 and head03 all bind head01's tint mask orc_head01_m.tex.

A color setup (Model/SimpleModelColorSetup, 465 records) holds entries of _StructuresSimpleModelColorEntry {SelectionWeight, Hue in degrees +- HueDeltaRange, Saturation / Value minimum and maximum 0-255, AppearanceTag}. Armor

Designs and pieces

A design's ModelPicker is a list of _StructuresInventoryArmorModelPicker {SimpleModelPiece, RequiredBodyType}. 0x61BC24(design, body type) returns the first entry whose RequiredBodyType is the member's body type or 0 (any body). A design with no piece for that body type empties the slot. Which armor fits a captain is decided by the body type at +0x00, not by the body piece's name. On 162 living captains, 870 of 870 armor pieces in slots 7-12 whose name carries a body family carried the captain's +0x00 family; those captains include 10 Stocky and Olog captains drawn in the any-body Orc_Buff_Machine_Body. Of the 926 armor-named pieces, 847 are put on some body by a shipped design; the rest are Easterling sets and DLC character accessories.

Sets are named design families AD_<Set>_<Slot>, many with Bling variants (21 *Bling* designs). A Bling piece is the same model as its plain twin with a different mesh-part table (see Shoulder pads and appearance tags below). The nine tribe sets cover all four bodies, except that Regal has no Olog legs.

A piece record carries Model, Tint (its tint layers), MeshIndexPairs, AppearanceTag, BaseWeight and ArmorDataDynamicBundle (its geometry bundle). A design also names its material bundle (DynamicBundle, a link to a DynamicResource/BundleDefinition, for example Black_Base), its follower and enemy color overrides, and an OutOfMemoryBackups design, used when the piece does not fit the gear budget (AD_Black_Shoulders names AD_Sauron_Shoulders; see Resource streaming, "The gear budget").

Items carry the armor

Beyond the SimpleModel's own pieces, a captain wears armor only as items. The member's inventory inv = FM+0x50 is laid out as follows:

Offset Meaning
+0x00 + 8 x slot item instances of the 10 weapon slots (by the look's ItemSlot; see Items)
+0x50 + 8 x slot item instances of the 15 armor slots (by ArmorSlot; accessor 0xFCA450 asserts slot < 15)
+0xC8 / +0xD0 the color list: entries {u8 source, u32 palette slot, u32 ARGB}
+0xE0 / +0xE8 the look-record list: Inventory/Items records with a body change or material overrides

The give path 0x355474(inv, fm, item, source, appearance), 181 instructions long, works in five steps:

  1. It refuses the item (returns false) when the member's combat tree's CharacterType fails the item's RequiredCharacterType / ExcludedCharacterType.
  2. For each FlairColors entry (Inventory/Flair/Color {ColorType < 17, ColorRecord}), it gets or creates the color-list entry of that slot (0x1C10E54) and rolls it in 0x1C11A74: the replace rule below, a weighted entry pick and an HSV roll seeded by FM+0x168.
  3. An item with an ItemBuilder becomes an instance. The build 0x649790 draws one WeaponData look and then one ArmorData design uniformly, sets +0x48 to the design and +0x30 to its ArmorSlot (15 when none), and the source goes to +0x100. 0x61B868 then places the instance in its slot and adds its item's AppearanceTags. The give returns whether the equip succeeded and stops there.
  4. An item without a builder is filed in the look-record list (once, 0x1162D40, de-duplicated by 0x7D9234) when it has a BodyModelChange or any PassiveMaterialOverrides.

What the save keeps of an item. Pieces on other skeletons

The piece-to-rig table

A worn piece's vertices name bones by the piece skeleton's own numbers. The engine turns them into the wearer's through one table per (rig, piece): the binding object [part+0x80] (vt 0x1DC7220, 0x2F0 bytes, constructor 0x702870, find-or-create 0x1CC280, key at +0x10 = hash(rig) | hash(piece) << 32), whose u16 vector sits at +0x38 / +0x40. Every wearer on that rig shares it, and one piece on two rigs has two objects with the same high half of the key (12 models, 50 parts).

Each part's render object ([part+0x48], vt 0x1FFE3E8) holds a copy that the draw reads directly: the u16 table at +0x148, the u8 table at +0x150 and the count at +0x158, inside a member struct at +0x10 (vt 0x1DAB810, constructor 0x1BE460, which is why its own code addresses them as +0x138 / +0x140 / +0x148). The setter 0x1BFBB8 frees the old copy, keeps the u16 table and derives the u8 one from its low bytes. The render object's vf60, 0x4C11A4(render, u16*, count), copies a caller's table and queues 0x1BFBB8 on the render ring 0x272E8D8 (the same ring and commit, 0x251E00, as vf47, the material setter). The callers of vf60 are the part's resource-arrived callback (0x66C714 -> 0x1BD968) and vt_0x1FE1A18::vf1 0x1BE1C0, both passing the binding's vector. An empty vector means no table at all (0x6D5CE4).

The table comes from walking the piece's bone tree: a bone takes the wearer's number for the same name only when it and every ancestor exist on the wearer, and otherwise keeps its raw number. That rule reproduces the engine's tables on 128 of 128 entries (a by-name rule matches 57, the identity mapping 122). The table is not in the .mesh file. Talion's skeleton runs Null > pNull > Pelvis and no other rig has pNull, so on any other rig every bone below it keeps its raw number. The arms then bend incorrectly, and raw numbers past the wearer's bone count (32 of a Talion suit's on a 96-bone rig) index past his palette. A piece that finds no bone of its own on the rig, such as Talion's hair, comes out as the identity mapping and gets no table. Havok cloth reads the wearer's bones through the same table (see Cloth).

The skeleton object

The piece's skeleton is [[part+0x70]+0x10], and the wearer's is [[[part+8]+0x1D8]+0x10] (see Animation):

Offset Meaning
+0x38 nodes, 0x40 bytes each: byte 0 the parent (0xFF = root), byte 1 its own index, byte 3 the child count
+0x40 bone count
+0x58 / +0x60 {u32 hash, u32 index} pairs sorted by hash (P919 of the name; pNull = 0xB8AD19F7)

Nodes are in level order: a parent comes before its children (3,047 of 3,047). Pairs whose index is at or past the bone count exist and are not bones (435 of 3,482), and a complete skeleton has exactly one in-range pair per bone (27 of 27).

The humanoid piece skeletons share one bind pose. Level of detail and material cells

Each mesh part's row in the piece skeleton is {u8, u8, f32 distance, u32, i32 -1}. On every piece read (7), the u32 is the index, in the Model's SolidMaterial list, of the material the part draws at that distance. Shipped orc armor uses cell 0 for LOD0 and cell 1 beyond (main parts [0, 1, 1, 1, 1, 0] over 0 / 500 / 800 / 1200 / 2000 / 7500 cm), and the mesh's LOD table sends both of the first two distances to the LOD0 submesh. LOD0 vertices are 64 bytes and carry a second UV set, which gear_lod0 draws: every vertex shader of gear_lod0.fxo (3) and gear_alphatest_lod0.fxo (15) takes TEXCOORD1, and those of gear_lod1.fxo (7) do not. Distance vertices are 60 bytes. Orc_Buff_FaceCape_Chest, the Druid legs (60-byte vertices at all four LODs) and the Druid pants keep cell 0 at LOD0 with no second UV set, so they vanish in close-up. A skeleton can list more LOD rows than its mesh has parts (the Thrall shoulders list 28 for 14 parts, some Head03 heads 54 for 24).

Shoulder pads and appearance tags

A shoulder piece's MeshIndexPairs are ranges of the model's mesh parts. Each is a _StructuresSimpleModelMeshIndexPair {MinIndex, MaxIndex, RequiredAppearanceTag[], ExcludedAppearanceTag[]}; the database holds 4,447 of them. 0x1B37F0 keeps the pairs whose required tags are all present in the appearance and whose excluded tags are absent, and picks one uniformly from a copy of the visual's seed (+0x98). 0x1BD724 packs the chosen (min, max) at piece +0x56 (a min of 1 becomes 0), and the material object's vf38 0x1BD764 queues it on the render ring 0x272E8D8; the render side keeps it at (material+0x10)+0xB8. On orc gear, the part a vertex belongs to is round(uv1.x x 10): the rule is exact on the Slaughter parts 1-5 at all four LODs and on 26 of 29 Buff shoulder pieces. The mesh's arenas are LODs, not parts.

The tags are Tag_ShoulderBoth, Left_Shoulder and Right_Shoulder, given by items (Both_Shoulders, the Only_* items, some chests and scars). A Bling piece maps 2-2 to 1-2, 3-3 to 3-4 and 2-3 to 1-4 (parts 1 and 4 are the plates). Of 132 orc and Olog shoulder pieces, 80 have no both-sides layout.

Tags are added by SetPieceSlot, by item equip (0x61B868 and 0x6E9174, from an item's AppearanceTags), by the class kit at the start of a build (0x6FEA04) and by color entries; they are removed per piece on a swap. 0x2EA18C adds a count, and 0x6E90F4 subtracts a count and removes an entry at zero. A build adds the kit's and the colors' tags again without clearing, so counts grow with each build. The vector is saved (smc_storage_appearancetags, P919 hashes; the save loader is 0x1C67944). Tags also select weapon models through _StructuresInventoryItemModel.OverrideModels (0x3FF344): 0x1BFB1FC picks such a model when the wearer holds every required tag.

Scars

A scar is a set of items, not a model slot. AddScar 0x7BB8C4 gives one random variant of a Combat/Scar/Type and its helmet item at source 10, and re-dresses the captain. A variant's parts are decals on the head or the body (scar materials only), hides, and armor items that bare or replace the head, empty the chest or arms, or swap the body for its scar twin. Captain scars and the return from death describes the full system, which parts arrive and which never do, and how a return from death picks its scar.

Color on screen

Tint layers

A piece's Tint is a Model/SimpleModelTint (76 records) that maps the tint mask's R, G, B and A channels to palette slots, where index 16 means unused. The records are named after the slots they read, and 74 of 75 named records agree: for example, Skin_Eye_Warpaint_Hair reads 0, 1, 4, 2 and Metal_Leather_FactionCloth_Trim 5, 6, 12, 9, while Skin_Eye_Warpaint_Metal reads Hair on A. Only skin layouts map a channel to Tattoo: 5 of the 76 (the orc body, the Machine bodies, two Easterling and the Gondorian heads; 14 pieces in all).

The writer 0x36B430 walks the visual's 23 pieces. For each piece with a Tint, it finds or creates the tint entry (0x4C3634), and for each channel below 16 (0x2EAD20), 0x2EAF10 -> 0x2EAFF4 writes the layer. When the palette entry's alpha is 0xFF, the layer gets amount 1.0 and color (R, G, B) / 255 (SetParamVector, handle slot 7); otherwise it gets amount 0.0 and no color record. The head gate 0x2EAF10 (59 instructions) gates palette entries 3 and 4 only: on the head (slot 1), it gates the tattoo layer by SimpleModel.TattooApplyToHead and war paint by PaintApplyToHead (true on 182 of 185 SimpleModels). SimpleModelPieces.Tint is read live by 0x36B430, 0x36B5D8 and 0x4C4974, and the layout's channels by 0x2EAD20; nothing copies them.

The second writer (0x36B5D8 -> 0x4C3F58 -> 0x78CD3C) applies a design's PlayerFollowerColorOverrides or SauronFollowerColorOverrides (list fields of Inventory/ArmorData), rolled in HSV, in an entry keyed by that design, which wins over the palette entry. Most of the 855 designs carrying such lists name human and elf colors (Ranger_*, Gondor_*, Eltariel_*, Haradrim_*). The caragor armor designs AD_Warg_Armor_Chest and AD_Warg_Rare_Armor_Chest name orc colors, and this writer is what paints caragor armor: on 5 of 5 armored caragors, layer A holds AA4425 on enemies and 5A8FAA on allies, entry 4 of the lists (Orc_Warpaint_Red / _Blue). An orc ally's colors do not come from this writer but from his tint items (see below).

The tint owner [visual+0xC0] holds the character's SimpleModel at +0x08 and a vector of 0x18-byte entries at +0x18 / +0x20: {SimpleModelTint, design (0 = base), handle}. An entry whose design is the slot's design wins over the base entry. Pieces that share a SimpleModelTint share one store, and a piece binds one store for all its materials: the Machine body's skin and its four plating materials read the same Skin_FactionWarpaint_Metal_Tattoo store. The handle class and its setters are described in Rendering. The store (0x28 bytes, constructor 0x4C38B8) holds its records {u32 type << 28 | hash, u32 size, data} at +0x00 / +0x08 (type 2 is a float3 k_c?TintColor, type 5 a float k_f?TintAmount), a reference count at +0x1C and a change stamp at +0x20.

The render cache. A piece's render node rebuilds its parameters only when one of its stores' stamps is newer than its last build. Values written into a store in place change nothing on screen until the stamp moves ahead of the counter the render thread keeps in its thread-local block (about 7,900 increments per second).

The blend. Each layer, in order R, G, B, A, moves the running color toward the tinted diffuse: c = c + mask x amount x (tint x diffuse - c), always from the original diffuse (as read in full in the three body pixel shaders that blend). Neither the factor nor the albedo is clamped from below (of the skeletal variant's 11 saturates, 8 clamp decal coordinates and 3 a wet / blood factor), so an amount above 1.0 extrapolates and the 8-bit target stores an undershoot as 0.

The store holds the palette byte / 255, but on screen a tint behaves as an sRGB-encoded color. A paint color chosen to equal its plate as a linear factor (6F5D4A) drew strokes at 0.40 / 0.34 / 0.30 of the plate (14,172 pixels measured). That is near what decoding the byte as sRGB first predicts, and far from the 1.0 a linear factor would give. Paint colors encoded as sRGB (B0A393, BBBFBA) blend into their plates. The gear shader also interpolates each mask layer's specular sample toward k_v?SpecularTint (reflectance, specular occlusion, roughness) by mask x k_f?TintAmount x k_fSpecularTintToggle x k_f?SpecularTintAmount. Gear materials declare the vectors (0.6667, 1, 0.5) with amounts 0, and no captain's tint store carries them.

Where the colors come from

The class roll fills palette entries 0-14. Entry 15, faction war paint, only ever comes from items, and so do most of an orc's armor colors. A FlairColors row (Inventory/Flair/Color {ColorType = palette slot, ColorRecord -> Model/SimpleModelColorSetup}) is rolled when the item is given (see above), so one repointed ColorRecord reaches every orc the engine dresses or re-dresses after it.

  • Grunts hold IT_Faction_<Enemy|Friendly>_TintItem, and captains hold IT_<Tribe>_Tribe_Tint or IT_<Tribe>_Tribe_Follower_Tint (Blackguard, Dark, Feral, Industrial, Marauder, Mystic, Slaughter, Terror, Warmonger, plus IT_Bruz_*).
  • Allies use Orc_Faction_Cloth_Blue (slot 12, Clothing_Color_01_Blue 5E89A5), Orc_Faction_Paint_Blue (15, 5A8FAA), Orc_Cloth_Friendly (7, Orc_Talion_Faction_Clothing 5B87A5), Orc_Metal_Friendly and Orc_Trim_Metal_Friendly (5 / 9, C2CCBE) and Orc_Leather_Friendly (6). Enemies use the _Red / _Enemy twins (Orc_Sauron_Faction_Clothing with red, black, green and purple entries; Orc_Metal_Enemy A09590).
  • Eighteen records color every orc's armor: for metal, Orc_Metal_Enemy / _Friendly / _Copper (Industrial) / _Silver (Mystic); for trim, Orc_Trim_Metal_Enemy / _Friendly / Orc_Trim_Gold (Marauder); for leather, Orc_Leather_Enemy / _Friendly / _Black / _Brown (Feral) / _YellowBrown (Slaughter) and Orc_FactionLeather_GreyFur_Range (Warmonger, slot 11); for cloth, Orc_Cloth_Enemy / _Friendly / _Black; and for side cloth, Orc_Faction_Cloth_Red / _Blue. The enemy grunts' leather setup Orc_Faction_Leather_Enemy has no entries, so every enemy grunt's leather ships untinted.
  • Beasts have every tint link of their SimpleModels empty, and no item colors them, so a caragor's palette slot 4 stays unset. Armor whose design carries color overrides (AD_Warg_Armor_Chest, above) is painted by the second writer; any other shows the white base of its strokes. Graug and drake pieces have no tint mapping at all: their paint is in their textures.

War paint on armor is a tint layer over a light neutral base. Under the paint channel the diffuse reads saturation 0.03 - 0.06 and 1.5 - 2.7 times the luminance of the texels around it (13 materials decoded), so an unset entry (amount 0) shows white marks. The paint channel differs by set: A carries faction paint on Beast, Industrial, Scavenger, Warmonger and the Sauron / Talion chests, and B on Commander, Destroyer, Marksman, Thrall, Tank and Feather. On the Metal_Leather_Cloth_Warpaint title pieces and the caragor armor, A carries war paint (slot 4). Faction cloth (12) covers a large share of Black (B 23%) and Mystic (B 26%). The grunt outfit has no faction-cloth layer; its plate paint is baked into faction_armor_d.tex. A stroke is also present in the specular map, so a painted texel on a metal plate is matte and shows its full albedo: faction_armor_s.tex reads R 0.72 / G 0.61 / B 0.68 in a stroke's core against 0.92 / 0.92 / 0.43 immediately outside, and R 170 / 255 is a dielectric.

Masks: what a layer can reach

A layer reaches only where its mask channel is set.

  • Blank masks. Of 431 character materials on the four gear shader trees, 114 bind a blank mask: 103 bind flatblack_alpha.tex (all four channels 0) and 11 bind flatblack.tex (R = G = B = 0 and A = 1). Among them, the big claw, the peg leg and the underclothes carry a tint store that the palette writes, yet still draw untinted. metalplates.mat / metalplateslods.mat are drawn by 18 Models: the four Metal Heads, the MetalJaw, MetalBandage and MetalCap helmets of the three orc bodies, the three olog helmets, and the Shovel and Snare weapons. Both materials use flatblack_alpha with every amount declared 0. The Metal Head (a scar's head, AD_Metal_Head, armor slot 11) is a single material object holding head01's face materials (cells 0-2), gore caps (3-4), eyes (5-6) and plates (7-8), so one store reaches the face and the plates alike. Monolith's own metal head, Orc_*_Machine_Head (Skin_Eye_Metal_FactionWarpaint), puts its metal on B (orc_machine_helmet_m sets B on 93.1% of texels). Each plate Model owns its material set (2,995 of 2,995 set primaries have their own instance). Every engine store bind uses index 0 (351 of 351 pairs on 119 pieces).
  • Plating with no alpha. The Machine plating masks are BC1 (alpha 1) or BC3 with alpha 1.0 on 262,144 of 262,144 texels, so a Tattoo layer at amount 1.0 on the Machine body's store covers the whole plating after Metal. The Machine body's tint is Skin_FactionWarpaint_Metal_Tattoo, with metal on B over 82.8% of the body plating, 91.0% of the shoulders and 86.1% of the pants; the arms and legs are 50.4% metal and 49.6% skin. The Machine head's tint is Skin_Eye_Metal_FactionWarpaint (its helmet mask is 100% B). The Tower body uses Skin_Metal_Leather_Metal, the Dragonborn Skin_Metal_Leather, and Flint, Tinder, Infested and Glutton the standard Skin_Cloth_Warpaint_Tattoo.
  • Skin keeps its sores. Inside the skin zone of orc_body001_m (R above 0.05, G below 0.3), R is 0.86 at the median and 0.55 / 0.67 / 0.72 at the 1st / 5th / 10th percentile (0.58 / 0.71 / 0.74 on the Machine body's mask). The low-R texels are the scabs, blisters and folds, which keep part of their own color under any skin tint: a black skin at amount 1.0 keeps 15.5% of the texture on average. G is the cloth zone (loincloth and wraps: 14.4% of the standard mask and 15.0% of the Machine's). On the Machine body, G is faction war paint, which reads amount 0.0, so its wraps show the raw texture.
  • Accessories. In all, 42 orc and olog accessories form 22 families, 10 of them with no Tint. Orc_Buff_Spiders_Accessory has none and therefore no store. Its material (orc_spiders_accessory.mat, gear_alphatest_lod0) masks with flatblack.tex, so only the A layer could reach it. Its strands are an effect: thespider_spidersbody01.fxp, three PolyString nodes on Head and the forearms, whose color is the effect key's shared StringColor.

Eyes and glow

A captain's eyes are solid material slots 5 and 6 of his head piece's material object, one material per eye (see Characters for the slots). The eye materials are eye_orc, _black, _bright, _serpent, _undead, _cataracts, _cut, _metal and _crossed, eye_cursed and olog_eyes, all resident in a loaded world. eye_orc_spider ships only in a DLC bundle, and its own tint mask is blank (its red comes from its diffuse). No eye mesh ships. The eye shader tints the iris by the G layer (the eye palette entry) and writes an emissive value saturate((mask . k_vEmissiveTintChannelMask - range.x) / (range.y - range.x)) x k_fEmissiveScale. Orc heads ship range (0, 2) and scale 0.05 (0.35 on bright eyes, 0 on cataracts), so a fully masked pixel glows at half the scale. Olog heads have no eye glow.

The emissive class and row go into the G-buffer material alpha as (32 + row) / 255. Eyeballs always write row 7 (39 / 255, the generic diffuse row, which glows in the eye's own tint), and ordinary pixels 0 or 1. The rows index materials\0dev\engine\emissive_lut.texa (16 x 256, R11G11B10 float), whose header names them: 0 blackbody, 1 wraith, 2 balefire, 3 lit interior, 4 nazgul, 5 cursed, 6 zone map, 7 generic diffuse, 8 lava flow, 9 balrog ice, 10 / 11 blackbody, 12 blackbody test, 13 fire decal, 14 fire, 15 balefire. Each row ramps over the strength: it is near black below about 0.4, nazgul reaches (0, 1.06, 0.28) at 0.75 and wraith (0.18, 1.06, 1.59), and rows 12-15 reach values in the hundreds. The head writes its row from k_nEmissiveLUTIndex (orc heads ship 7), and the EyeGlowLUT_* records set rows Fire 0, Wraith 1, Poison 2, Nazgul 4 and Cursed 5. Integer material parameters are stored as floats (EyeGlowLUT_Nazgul puts k_nEmissiveLUTIndex in its store as type 5, 4.0). Of 378 override records, only the five EyeGlowLUT_* set a row.

The brand. After its eight color decals, head_cin reads one more slice of the scar array: layer k_fBrandIndex, position k_vBrandPosition, rotation k_fBrandRotationCosSin and scale k_vBrandScale. Its strength, the slice's alpha x k_fBrandBrightness, is written as the emissive strength with row k_nBrandLUTIndex (the eyes' encoding). The Olog head01 / head02 materials ship it at brightness 0, layer 15, position (0.33, 0.35), scale 0.909, rotation -5 and row 1 (wraith). The title look "Brand of the Dark Lord" turns on HasBrand, DarkTalionBrand and EyeGlowLUT_Nazgul. The body shaders write no emissive: their G-buffer alpha is a subsurface class (k_vSubSurfaceID), and the body materials' k_vEmissiveTintChannelMask is (0, 0, 0, 0) and is read by none of the three body pixel shaders.

A title's eye look is an item (58 items, given by 64 titles). Its MaterialSetVariants swap only the eyeball (19 variants, all on the head), its PassiveMaterialOverrides paint the rest, and its FlairColors set skin and eye colors. An undead captain's glowing eyes are not the eye material but the I_Am_Undead item's PassiveFXSequencePicker: UndeadFX -> necro_undead_ambient effects with "Left Eye Star" / "Right Eye Star" particles on the eye bones, started by the item handler 0x1C0E0C -> 0x79BFF4 (see Visual effects). The fire helmet is the same kind of look: Fire_Helmet names FireHat -> firehat.fxp on Head, whose flame nodes take their color from a slice of the emissive table (k_fFireLUT). Of 5,618 items, 21 carry an effect picker.

Material overrides and decals

AddMaterialOverride 0x57AF70(visual, record, n) and RemoveMaterialOverride 0x57D530(visual, record, n) put an Inventory/MaterialOverride (378 records) on a drawn character and remove it. Add does nothing unless the visual's byte +0xB2 is set and the visual has a skeleton (+0x90). The list visual+0xC8 holds 0x38-byte entries {+0x00 decal slot, +0x08 record, +0x10 count, +0x18 handle, +0x20 vector}. The same record again only raises the count, and a remove at zero unbinds and frees the entry.

A new entry's parameters come from the record. Each _StructuresInventoryMaterialParam names either a parameter picker (such as ScarLayer, ScarPosition, ScarRotation or ScarScale, with 16 names each) or a plain name, and ParamValueType sets the value's type: 0 float, 1 a float-pool pair, 4 int. The entry is placed in the lowest decal slot that no entry of the same piece group sharing that picker uses, and is bound at index 0 of the piece's material, behind the stores already there. When two stores bound to one material carry the same key, the first in the material's list wins. With the engine's amount ahead of a second store's, the second store changed nothing; with the order reversed, it took effect. Two overrides on the same piece and key therefore do not combine. The head and body shaders read decal slots 0-7, so an entry filed past slot 7 is not drawn.

Body decals need the scar material. Normal bodies draw orc_body001(v2).mat / olog_body001.mat (body_noscar.fxo, no decal slots). The scar twins (orc_body_scar, orc_body_stk_scar, olog_body_scar and their LODs, body_lod0/1.fxo) use the same textures and meshes plus eight decal slots. A decal store holds k_fScarLayerN, k_fScarRotationN, k_fScarTintN (floats) and k_vScarPositionN, k_vScarScaleN, k_fScarRotationNCosSin (type 11, float2). A decal of scale s covers a UV square of side 1 / (4s), with no tiling. The decal reads the scar array's color, normal and specular slices. k_fScarTintN blends its color toward the same decal tinted by the body's layers (0 gives the raw decal, 1 a decal tinted like the skin under it): decal = raw + ScarTint x (raw x layers - raw), unclamped, so a value above 1 extrapolates. Decals 1-7 composite over one another (total += (1 - total) x a) and are normalized by that total before the tint; decal 0 is not.

Weapons and shields

Weapons use the armor shaders (nemesis\gear_lod0/1.fxo, gear_alphatest_lod0/1.fxo) with the same tint parameters, so a palette color lands on a weapon wherever its mask is set, and almost nowhere is. An Inventory/EquippedWeaponData.Model names an item model {ActiveNode / InactiveNode, Models (-> ModelRecord, a Wep_* Model, MinMeshIndex / MaxMeshIndex), MaterialOverrides, Tint, ApplySummonedByTribeMaterialOverride}. The engine makes a tint store only where Tint is set, on 20 of 1,903 item models (16 shields, such as Regal Metal_Leather_Trim and RangerKiller Cloth_Leather_FactionWarpaint, the throwing spear and the war banner). Of 77 orc weapon and shield materials, 64 bind flatblack_alpha.tex (every layer masked off) and 9 their own mask (the Regal and RangerKiller shields, both throwing spears, the quiver and three war banners). Over all weapon materials, 68 of 306 are masked.

  • Parts are the mesh's own material ids. The id is uv1.x in tenths, one per vertex, on 53 of 87 orc weapon meshes; 11 hold real coordinates there and 23 have no second UV set. The vertex shader passes it on, and the pixel shader picks the detail layer round(id - 1) from characters\modelcharacters\global\detail\detail_array_01_*.texa, whose header names the slices: 1 leather, 2 metal, 3 cloth, 4 bone, 5 skin, 6 cloth 2, 7 chain, 8 noise. gear_lod1 and gear_alphatest_lod1 do not pass the second UV set. The same channel is the armor pieces' mesh-part number (see Shoulder pads and appearance tags above).
  • Metal is the specular map's R. It is 170 / 255 on every dielectric and rises toward 255 on metal (63 of 77 materials have both; 6 are all metal). A metal texel's albedo tints only its reflections, so a colored polished blade reads dark with colored glints. k_bSpecGAlphaTest, used by 3 of the 11 alpha-tested weapons (the war banners), cuts the alpha by the specular G after the tints. The blood decal's position is seeded from the R and G tint colors (frac(sum(R) x 8), frac(sum(G) x 6)). k_fBloodValue (shipped 0), k_fBloodAlpha and k_cBloodColor drive the decal in gear_lod0 modes 0 and 2; meshtype5 does not read them.
  • A weapon's drawn model is reached through a chain of objects. The visual component ([char+0x2348], on an Army copy [stage+0x768], vt 0x1FD63F0) holds the holder of each slot at [component+0x38 + slot x 0x28] (holder vt 0x1DB1F68, or 0x1DCA710 for a shield carrier's slot 0). The holder keeps its elements at +0x148 / +0x150 (vt 0x1DB4F58, an object reference: +0x08 next, +0x10 previous, +0x18 self, +0x20 target). An element's +0x20 is the built object (vt 0x1F782C0), whose +0x120 is the model (vt 0x1DB4948, empty while not drawn). The model holds the wielder at +0xE0, the material object at +0x120 and the mesh path at +0x170 (54 of 55 models on 27 Army copies, 16 of 16 world characters). A material object holds one material per LOD (orc_metalshield.mat, orc_metalshieldlods.mat), and an item can pick a variant (orc_metalshieldv4.mat). A weapon's render node watches the character's own store, which carries only k_fDissolve (its handle is at [skel+0x238] in the world).
  • Two-model looks. A look whose Models list holds two item models draws both. Orc_1HS_EliteSwordX2, for example, uses RightHand / Ber_R_sheath and LeftHand / Ber_L_sheath, both drawing Wep_Orc_1HS_EliteSword with no mirror or offset (18 of 18 two-model looks in hand holders had both built). Every Defender look is a two-model shield look (41 of 41 Orc_, Gondorian_ and Easterling_ shield looks): model 0 is his weapon (RightHand / Socket_Sheath_Def01) and model 1 the shield (Shield_L_Attach / Socket_Sheath_Def02). In the world his item builds the shield holder, whose vf35 0x6E517C calls the hand holder's 0x3FCF70 (destroy and build every model) and then reads Items.ShieldWeaponData. Over 1,748 looks and 1,909 item models, the active sockets are RightHand 1,053, LeftHand 673, Shield_L_Attach 85 and RightDagger 16, followed by creature and test sockets. The rest sockets are Sheath_Socket 383, Dagger_Sheath_Socket 380, Socket_Sheath_Def01 / Def02 79 each, L_sheath 51 and Ber_R / Ber_L_sheath 11 each. No right-hand shield socket exists. LeftHand sits on L_Weapon, which a Defender's clips never move (200 cm from his L_Hand on his stage pose); his clips carry Shield_L.
  • The drawn mesh is the model chain, not the name. The chain runs EquippedWeaponData.Model -> _StructuresInventoryItemModel.Models -> _StructuresInventoryModel.ModelRecord -> a Model record. Over 1,763 looks (40 with no model), BlackHand_Sword, Sauron_Mace, BlackHand_Bow, BlackHand_2 and BlackCaptain01_Belroth draw the broom prop Wep_Orc_Misc_Broom, and Raptor_Body draws a whole caragor. The boss weapons (such as Sauron_1HB_FlangedMace, Sauron_1HS_ElvenSwordT3 and Nazgul_1HS_BlackBlade) have an empty InactiveNode, so they never sheathe. In all, 1,742 looks reference 1,852 item models, 10 of them shared; 1,111 share one descriptor run (21 fields).

Grunts

Grunts are not faction members and get no member build. It skips the player, recognized by the combat object [component+0x440] (vt 0x1F995E0), whose +0x50 is set on the player only. When a changed look shows

Residency. A piece draws only when its geometry and material bundles are resident. Both are requested by the gear load request of an equipped item (0x61B868 asks the gear budget manager through 0x4B4C7C; see Resource streaming). A piece no design lists has no material bundle to request: AD_Machine_Body lists only Orc_Buff_Machine_Body (body type 0), so the Slim and Stocky Machine bodies, which have their own geometry bundles, have materials (machine_body_m.bndl) that nothing requests. Easterling pieces' designs list body type 12 only and name no material bundle; their shared easterling_outfit.mat ships in easterlings_base, easterling_buff_heavy_arms_g and flair\grunt\desert_grunt_m. A head is the same: its face record's bundle and its family's _g bundle must both be resident (see Head, hair and helmet above).

The appearance object and the body. 0x61BAD4 and the build write only the appearance object (SetPieceSlot with no visual). A drawn body changes through SetPieceSlot with its visual and through the equip path's load request.

0x62EB14 (often labeled UpdateVisualModel) is an army refresh, not a character refresh: it takes an army context and that army's summary container ([ctx+0x30]), rebuilds the container's summaries (0x62BC44, refilling each from its member through 0x62BF54) and asks the world UI to refresh the army's stage (0x62E30C). All 7 of its 12 call sites that were examined pass an army context: the walk over every army 0x1B5D66C, fort-upgrade removal 0x1B5E39C, 0x1C1B22C, 0x1C1DB10, the promotion events 0x1C1D828 (with a temporary container), the console rebuild, and 0x7A97DC, which first replaces the army's summary container (0x62AFD4) and is called after the faction system initializes and from sim-event generation. A member is not a valid first argument. The rebuild's first instructions (mov rax,[rdx+0xD8] / mov [rcx+0x9F50],rax) store the context's +0xD8 in the container's +0x9F50 before anything is tested. Passed a member and his 0x1C0-byte appearance object in place of a context and a container, it therefore writes a pointer 0x9F50 bytes past the appearance object.

The Army screen

The Army screen draws each captain from his army's summary (0x1A8 bytes, one per member, in the summary container), not from the member (see User interface for the stage objects). The refill 0x169168(summary, fm) copies the key {FM+0x178, FM+0x174} (+0x88), the class (+0x138), combat tree (+0x130), role (+0x140), personality (+0x148), tribe (+0x150), rarity (+0x178), level and flags. It rebuilds the trait and marker lists, and copies the appearance into [summary+0x40] with 0x2EBA60 and the inventory into [summary+0x30]. Both copies come from the mock member when FM+0x2E0 is set (0x75D2FC / 0x75D314), so such a captain shows the older look (see The Nemesis system).

The per-captain redraw is 0x19C395C(world UI [base+0x26FFB60], &key). It resolves the army's stage (0x455EDC; armies of one region share one stage object), the summary (0x1B5C0F8) and the stage character (0x1A28FAC), and runs the character's rebuild UI_CWorldUICharacter 0x627F40(object, summary, 0, 0). The game calls it after a Training Order (0x1C51630). It draws from the summary as it is, so a summary that was not refilled redraws the old look, and the open screen also keeps its own summary copies per army (0x16FC1C). The screen builds a new character copy, with new tint handles from the palette, each time a captain is shown: one captain received three copies in a row, then one about every 40 seconds while he stayed in view. A tribe or armor change rebuilds the copy too, so a value written into one copy's material does not last.

Shipped quirks

  • Head03 is cut content. The head03 name is carried by 30 pieces and 30 face records: 29 *_Head03_C## heads over the three orc bodies, and Orc_Buff_Head03_G. The family is never rolled (0 of 557 captains, against 206 head01 and 293 head02), and its family bundle orc_head03_g.bndl (12,404,972 bytes, holding the six head03 materials, its textures, and beard and piercing materials) is linked by no record. Its diffuse orc_head03_d.tex is a flat orange placeholder with a black "3" in all three copies the engine reads: the bundle's BC1 section, the high-mip .tex0 and the HD .texuhd tier (a texture's header names its sibling tier). The sculpts, normal and specular maps and materials are finished, on the shared head UV template. Of its meshes, 16 (every Stocky one, three Slim, three Buff and the family mesh) are skinned on a 139-bone face skeleton with five extra leaf bones under Head (L_Ear, R_Ear, NoseUp, MouthUp, MouthLo) that no live face skeleton has; the live ones hold 134 bones, in head02's order. Three heads draw their eyes with the wrinkle skin material (cell 0), several LODs carry invalid rows or are skinned to the wrong bones, and three Buff heads ship their head sculpt as a part named Beard_mash2. Head03 carries no MeshIndexPairs and no stubble, piercing or ear burn parts.
  • Head geometry formats. Head LOD0 / LOD1 index buffers are tessellation patches (head_lod0 is a tessellation shader). A head part's palette is a stack of strictly ascending runs of at most 64 bone indices, and vertex lanes use slots 0-63 of the first run.
  • Model.Scale is not a piece property: its only copier 0x56E870 fills the creation parameters of objects spawned from a Model record.
  • No shipped record references the items behind 69 armor pieces (among them the Druid pants and legs, Underclothes, Leather shoulders, the Buff Defender set, and Easterling heavy and plate pieces). No archive ships a mesh for 15 cut pieces (among them the Buff Defender set, two Celebrimbor rings and a cinematic hair). A record count does not see world scripts: Sauron_Gondor1 and Vines_Accessory are given by mission scripts.

Open questions

  • How does a spawned body pick up a rebuilt appearance object, and does the engine support a body type change on a loaded character? A live comparison of [FM+0x2D8] with the visual's +0xB8 before and after a level-up would settle the first.
  • Where is a tint color decoded from sRGB to linear? The store holds byte / 255, but the screen behaves as if the byte were sRGB. A frame capture of one known color through the gear shader would settle it.
  • Does the engine read a skeleton's LOD row u32 as the material cell? The shipped pattern fits it on 7 pieces; a capture of a part's bound material at two distances would settle it.
  • Does an override that carries a key the tint store also carries (k_fEmissiveScale of an eye-glow record) win, as it appears to, against the first-store rule measured on armor? Reading the head material's store list order with both bound would settle it.
  • How does the draw compare a vertex's part number (round(uv1.x x 10)) with the range that MeshIndexPairs stores at (material+0x10)+0xB8? The comparison on the render side is not traced.
  • A material parameter the shader does not read (k_vEmissiveTintChannelMask on the body, k_fBrandRotation) never reached the constant buffer when added to a store. Does the engine update only the parameters a shader declares? That path is not traced.
  • How does Rogue_BodyTattoo (no material override, so the give path files nothing) apply the Rogue body, and which items carry the _LOD painted variants?
  • How do the body-animation marker items (Personality_UniqueBodyAnim_*_IT, no payload) reach the animation, and how are the ChildItemPicker tint items (IT_Faction_Enemy_Tint) resolved, since the give path reads neither?
  • Cloaks made for Talion's skeleton hang some cloth from bones turned 150-178 degrees from any bone a man has (the Rhovanion cloaks have 15 such anchors each; 43 of 53 cloaks on his skeleton have none). The presumed cause is that Havok's skin operators keep Talion's inverse binds; a posed capture of one such cloak on another rig would settle it.
