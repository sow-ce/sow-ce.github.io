Characters, the player and the camera

The character object and its key fields; the player; the camera pipeline and the copy the screen reads; near-camera dissolve; the character controller with its collision layers and trigger volumes; character size with the scale component and the live capsule swap; AI spacing and attack reach; the player's movement record, immediate teleport and model change with its build gate; Wraith World and its exit switches; the wraith companion model; weapons and holders, runtime material slots and render IDs; mounts; Talion's material controller; Sauron's two forms and their keyed effects; and birds.

Every character is a CCharacter with a body, a controller and a capsule in the Havok world, a scale component that sets its size, and a visual whose pieces, weapons and materials the engine builds from records and items. This chapter follows the object, the camera, the controller and the capsule, size and reach, the player's movement, teleport and model change, Wraith World and Talion's material controller, then Sauron's two forms and the birds.

The character object

Every character, including the player, NPCs and creatures, is a CCharacter. AI characters are the NPC class (vt_0x1FB3640, 0x3180 bytes; see AI), and the player is a CPlayerObj (vt_0x1F96818).

The player is [base+0x2797808], the first entry of the player list [base+0x2A34C68]; the client shell [base+0x26FFA98] holds it at +0x888. The camera

owner = [[[base+0x26FFAA8]+0x6CB0]+0x6728 + i*8]   (vt_0x1FD99F8; the local player's entry has +0xDEA8 set)
owner+0xDEA8 -> the camera modifier data (md)
owner+0x5F0  -> the modifier list (sentinel; +0x5F8 first node; node = modifier + 0x10, next = node + 8;
                modifier +0x08 owner, +0x20 its GameDB record, +0x28 state, 2 = finished or skipped)
md+0x66C position, +0x678 rotation quaternion, +0x690 / +0x694 horizontal / vertical FOV (radians)
md+0x650 / +0x688 the previous frame's transform / lens

In a frame (0x2FC6B8), 0x51BF3C(md, dt) prepares from the previous camera and the focus. 0x51BCA8 then runs each modifier's vf7 on md, validating after each, and the finalizer 0x51BE7C stores the result into the output object md+0x768 (+0x3A4 transform, +0x3C0 start transform, +0x3F4 / +0x3F8 lens). The screen reads that stored copy, not the live md. The chain 0x2FEA2C -> 0x51AC00 -> 0x51AC30 -> 0x4E0CFC copies it to player+0x3420, which drives the view, camera transitions and the lens. The live md is the camera's state for the next frame. The gameplay camera Camera/Behavior::Camera_Primary has 12 modifiers (Adjustables, Lens, SetRoot, SmootherBegin, Distance, OrbitControl, AvoidObstacles, Pitch, Yaw, Roll, Smoother, Collide). Only the collision modifier (vt_0x1DB5FE0) calls SetCameraPos 0x51C394. Presentation cameras are the *_BS / *_BR modifier families. While a menu is open the gameplay camera ([[player+0x2F90]+0xA8]) stays where it was, and the menus have their own camera (see User interface, "The Army camera").

The debug camera manager [base+0x2A86860] (vt_0x22F4A90, a state machine; built at client start by 0x17CD2EC, read by the screen copy 0x4E0CFC) takes a mode from DebugCamera.FreeCamControls (handler 0x17CFF54, which stores "Cinematic", "ClipMode" or "Marketing" as 0 / 1 / 2 at +0x118). DebugCamera.Track (0x17D0074) follows a named object.

Near-camera dissolve. Camera/CharacterDissolve (34 records: hide and fade radii and lerps) is set by camera behaviors (setter 0x1567E8, controller +0x240) and by presentation elements (1,380 of 3,548 set Dissolve.Settings, controller +0x248). 0x6C4E60 builds two capsules from the camera along its forward axis. The classifier 0x30A368 -> 0x309128 -> 0x308BE0 tests them against each body's capsules and returns show, fade or hide (a camera inside a body forces hide), unless the character's forced mode overrides it. The forced mode is 3 (the volume decides), 0 (always show) or 2 (always hide); it is set by presentations' HideActors / ExcludeActors and by HideAllCharacters 0x19B43B4. The result drives the material constant k_fDissolve (0x3093F4); at 1.0 the visible bit clears. Every result reads one table on [player+0x2F90] (vt_0x1F67370): +0x258 show {0.0, 0.2}, +0x260 hide {1.0, 0.1} and +0x268 fade {0.76, 0.2}. The table is copied once when the player is initialized.

The character controller

The controller is reached as ctrl = [[[player+0x2F88]+0x08]+0x28]: the movement object (char+0x24A0; the player's is vt_0x22F7738) holds at +0x08 a controller holder with no vtable, and the holder's +0x28 is the controller in use (one per character, 94 live in one scene). The controller is vt_0x1DB4DE0, with +0x38 the owner, +0x40 the hknpCharacterProxy (vt_0x1DBDA20: +0x3C filter info, +0x80 position in meters, +0xB0 velocity in m/s), +0x81 the support state (2 = standing), +0xE4 the collision filter and +0xEC gravity on. Its vtable slots are 5 SetPosition (cm), 6 SetVelocity, 8 Step (through the integrator 0x399F18), 11 SetGravityEnabled 0x3F1900, 12 IsGravityEnabled, 13 refresh support, 20 SetCollisionFilter 0x3F14B4 (which writes the controller's and the proxy's filter) and 24 CopyFrom. The controller object is replaced during play (across a death or respawn, and by every capsule rebuild, below), so it is reached through the chain on every use.

The proxy. The hknpCharacterProxy holds its shape at +0x28 (an hknpCapsuleShape on 17 of 17 live proxies) and the physics world at +0x40. +0x50 .. +0x8F is its transform: three rotation columns, then the position at +0x80 (w = 0), which equals the character's +0xAC / 100 (0.0 cm apart on the player). +0x90 / +0xA0 are the minimum and maximum corners of a box around the shape, relative to the position (0.73 x 1.89 m up to 0.99 x 2.56 m for orcs and humans, 3.5 x 6.16 m for the largest creature in one scene). The integrator 0x399F18 (controller slot 8) turns the velocity into the step's displacement, +0xC0 = +0xB0 x dt (dt from the step information +0x08), casts the capsule from +0x80 to +0x80 + +0xC0, and keeps the velocity it started from in +0xD0. A value written to +0xB0 is therefore what the next step moves by. +0x120 is the up vector (0, 1, 0). The settings that follow are the same on all 17 proxies, so they are not per character:

Offset Value Use in the integrator
+0x130 0.05 added to +0x134 for the cast tolerance; also multiplied by 0.5 in a later step
+0x134 0.1 added to +0x130 for the cast tolerance
+0x138 10.0
+0x13C 50 (u32) added, with 10, to the manifold size +0x20 to size the plane buffer
+0x140 10.0 passed to the solver as the w of its input vector
+0x144 / +0x148 100.0 / 100.0
+0x14C 1.0
+0x150 10 (u32) the bound of the cast loop

An NPC's proxy does not walk with him. The player's proxy stands on him (0.00 cm), but an NPC's is usually parked while the movement step moves him. Of 96 bodies that walked more than 20 cm in a 148-second sample, the proxy moved 0 cm on 83, kept up for more than half the distance on 8 and fell in between on 5. With the game paused, 0 of 140 proxies sat within 1 cm of their character and 6 within 10 cm, with gaps of up to 160 m. Parked proxies often sit on round numbers. For an NPC the wall test is therefore the cast shape: the movement step 0x41C5C8 tests that [holder+0x08] exists (0x41DA2B) and sweeps it (0x41DA4D, through 0x1BD564C, a one-instruction jump to 0x36BFD0).

Collision layers. The world's collision filter is the object at [hknpWorld+0x480] (vt_0x22C2530, an engine subclass of hknpGroupCollisionFilter): two bodies collide when table[layerA] & (1 << layerB) (table at filter +0x24, symmetric). The player's proxy is on layer 7 (row 0x0030003D); every AI proxy is on layer 1 (row 0x00000001, the world only). Hit capsules use layer 6. Every character also carries a sensor body on layer 28 (filter 0x1C) exactly at its position, built by 0x3EE110 from the class's raw CapsuleRadius at scale 1.0, so it never follows size. The trigger rows are 27 = 0x10000001 (layers 0 and 28), 28 = 0x48000001 (0, 27, 30) and 30 = 0x10000001 (0, 28). The world's body array is [hknpWorld+0x28] (15,360 entries of 0xB0: +0x30 position in meters, +0x60 shape, +0x6C filter info with the layer in bits 0-4, +0x70 id). About 4,300 bodies are live in a region.

Trigger volumes. Script triggers are Havok trigger bodies: the trigger class table at 0x2A61E48 (TriggerSpatialBase constructor 0x1A52C88, TriggerSpatialShape 0x65575C / vt_0x1DC1228, TriggerSpatialDims 0x65599C / vt_0x1DC1660). TriggerSpatialDims vf45 0x3DAD68 creates the trigger through the physics system on layer 27 (31 for corpse triggers); TriggerSpatialBase vf21 0x3DA8AC filters by script tags, the player, excluded character types, mount and rider. Layer 27 meets only layers 0 and 28, so triggers react to characters' sensor bodies. The levels place out-of-environment kill and teleport volumes this way (for example OOEKillVolumes / OOEKV_* under Minas Ithil and the fortress walls' *.OOETSS / *.OOE_SO script objects, which teleport the player back): a character under the world dies by trigger, not by height. The kill itself is CCharacter::Die 0x19B3EE0 (100,000 damage, the float at 0x202ECBC; ApplyDamageInternal 0x2E4BE8). The engine's own world extents (+-5 km / +-3 km at 0x26C6588) only assert.

Height predicates. PredicateHeightAttackerIncluded (evaluator 0xEDD5F0) measures the height above ground with a ray cast from the character's position down to 100,000 cm (0x77A6D0) and tests it against a Combat/Predicates/Height range (On_Ground 0 - 50 cm, Jumping 40 - 5,000 cm). Every ground ranged attack carries On_Ground; 1,320 compiled attacks use it, 318 of them AI. An attack's AnimationData.EnableCollision / EnableGravity (combat +0x683 / +0x686) override the movement component's collision scale (0x41BEB4) and gravity (0x41C558) while it runs. The engine also has a native spectator movement state (NativeState.SpectatingState, SpectatorSpeedMultiplier1/2 10 and 0.1).

The player clock [[base+0x2702548]+8]+0x18 stops in the pause menu and while the game is unfocused; [base+0x2702590] keeps running.

Character size, capsules and reach

The size roll

A captain's size comes from 0x7F8488(fm) -> 0x4E541C(class FM+0x278, army FM+0x180 or else FM+0x178, seed FM+0x168):

size = min + (max - min) * frac + ScaleAdditiveModifier
frac = u32((seed * 0xBB38435 + 0x3619636B) >> 32) / 4294967295.0     (the divisor at 0x202E808; + 0.0 at 0x202E584)
(min, max) = Character/Models.AIScaleRange of the class

ScaleAdditiveModifier is a float of the army record (Faction/Definition; it reads 0.1 on 7 of 7 live army records). The formula predicts two captains exactly: an Orc_Defender (range 1.05 .. 1.15, roll 1.1307, + 0.1 = 1.2307, his +0xC8 1.2307, capsule radius 49.23 cm) and an Orc_Archer (0.9 .. 1.0, roll 0.9841, + 0.1 = 1.0841). The appearance roll shares the same fraction (see Captain appearance). A class whose maximum is not above its minimum returns the minimum. A body with no member takes its class from char+0x2B30 and its seed from char+0x2338 (0x4E3AD8 -> 0x4E3D4C, the same roll with no army bonus), and that function returns the global [0x267B1C0] instead whenever it is above 0. That global is a size override that ships as -1.0, which is off.

The callers of 0x7F8488 (7 of 7) are the body bind 0x40ED18, the refresh 0x4E3AD8, the player 0x19BD070 (from vt_0x1F96818 vf60 and SetPlayAsFactionMemberNPC), the Army summary 0x169168 (into the summary's +0x124), the sever 0x19D2858, 0x1B5AE88 and 0x1B5B0E8. ZTestVeryLargeOrcInfantry is not a mechanism: it is a Character/Models record that differs from Orc_Infantry in 9 of 102 fields, one of them AIScaleRange 2.0 / 2.0 (the shipped infantry has 0.9 / 1.1).

FM+0x180 is InitialFaction, the army a captain was born into, and so the record his size bonus comes from: a dominated captain keeps his birth army's bonus. The key name is in the document writer 0x1BC45A8 and the reader 0x1BC20B0. In a live game, 521 of 528 members hold the same army at +0x178 and +0x180, 7 differ, and none is null. Its readers are 0x7F8488, the two writers 0x594788 and 0x1BC45A8, and the member copy 0x1BB936C. It is written by the constructor 0x1BB4C1C, the loaders 0x1BC0F2C, 0x1BC20B0, 0x1BC21B4 (which reads faction_record into +0x178 and copies it into +0x180 at 0x1BC2238), 0x1BC04E4, 0x1C0E2F8 and the member builders. The save stores both armies by name hash. After the seed come 00 hash(FM+0x178) 00 hash(FM+0x180) (528 of 528 members; the 7 moved captains carry two different hashes, as in the live game). The writer 0x594788 -> 0x36E044 writes [rec+4] when [rec+1] & 2, and [rec+4] is P919(name) on 8 of 8 army records. The reader 0x1BC0F2C -> 0x17A9528 binary-searches the category. A private copy of a record that keeps the header therefore saves as the shipped record, and a load puts the shipped record back. A reader of a float field needs only [rec+8] (the descriptor run: +0x0C count, +0x0E link) and [rec+0x10] (the instance), as GetFloatFast 0x30E6B4 does; GetFloatByName 0x115B43C also reads [rec] and [rec+1].

The scale component

The scale component is comp = char+0x2738. The setter 0x4E3CF0(comp, size) stores +0x10 and +0x6C and calls 0x3EEBA8, but only when the size differs by more than 0.01 ([0x202E820]).

Offset Field
+0x00 u32 state, 0 = idle
+0x08 the character
+0x10 base size, what the body returns to
+0x14 applied size
+0x24 6 override slots of 0x0C: {u8 hasScale, u8 hasRate, f32 scale, f32 rate}
+0x6C / +0x70 blend target / rate per second
+0x74 CanScale (from char+0x27AC, getter 0x11498BC)

0x749790 recomputes the size. The lowest slot with a scale wins, otherwise the base. The rate is the slot's, otherwise 0.5 ([0x26756D8]) for an override and 0.1 ([0x26756DC]) back to the base. A target more than 0.01 away sets state 1; otherwise the target and rate are cleared and the state is 0. While the state is not 0, the tick 0x682674 (from 0x2F6A70) moves +0x14 by rate x dt through 0x3EEBA8. Set and clear are 0x11498C8(char, slot, scale, rate) and 0x1149838(char, slot); both refuse while CanScale is clear. 0x3FB0C8(comp, class) copies the class's CanScale (when false it empties every slot's flags and recomputes). It runs from NPC vf136 0x3FAFA4 (called by the bind 0x40ED18) and 0x3FA9C0, so the byte is re-armed at every bind.

What the setter reaches. 0x3EEBA8(comp, size) stores +0x14 and then reaches four places:

  • 0x402810 writes the character's +0xC8 after a finiteness test and calls 0x274C3C.
  • The character's vf84 (NPC 0x3EF034 -> the base 0x3EF0D0) rebuilds the ray-hit shape char+0x2AE0 (Model.RayHitDims) and the ranged one at char+0x26D0, calls [char+0x2528] slot 12 and writes +0x1088 .. +0x1090 and +0x111C. The NPC side adds [[char+0x2D30]+0x40] slot 23 and two tables indexed by the sighting slot char+0x2BF8.
  • 0x3EEB44 updates +0x1DC on the 10 attachment holders.
  • 0x1E52D4 writes the combat stat Float_CharacterScale (Combat/Stat/Variable/Float, loaded from [[GM+0x6CD8]+0x8300]).

In all, 135 functions read +0xC8 as a float (93 of them with offsets only a character has). They read it when needed, so pathing, fight circles, height predicates and teleports follow a resize with no further work. Camera collision (0x44DC90, Character/CameraCollisionShapeList) is built only at spawn.

The capsule swap

The capsule is double-buffered and swapped live by the engine, in four stages:

Stage Code What it does
build vf14 0x3F1304 -> 0x3F1548 -> 0x3F1D84 radius = max(dims.x, dims.z) x +0xC8, half height = dims.y x +0xC8; writes the pending dimensions, creates a controller at +0x30 with no proxy and queues it on W+0x61B8 (0x3F2FE4, locked); builds the cast shape only when [holder+0x08] is empty; moves the controller in use onto the character (slot 5 at 0x3F1F6F, the store at 0x41E0E6)
create the physics drain 0x1484C8 -> 0x5C0400 builds the hknpCharacterProxy from the construction info and stores +0x40: the controller is ready
swap the movement tick vf18 0x2FFAD0 -> 0x30186C vf24 0x711AA8 copies the ground state, the filter, and the old proxy's position and velocity; if the dimensions changed, pos.y += new half height - old half height (vf9, then vf5); committed = pending, in use = pending controller, +0x30 released, +0x59 = 1
retire vf2 -> 0x3F3678 -> W+0x61D0, drained by 0x3F27D4 the old controller is destroyed at a safe point; vf19 clears +0x59 in the same tick

0x24816C(&holder+0x08) releases and nulls the cast shape, so that vf14 builds a new one. Without it, the cast shape keeps the old size. Because vf14 first moves the old proxy onto the character, a parked proxy is pulled to its NPC, not the NPC to the proxy, and the new capsule stands at his x and z, lifted by the change in half height. Of the 21 functions that use the holder, only 0x30186C writes +0x59 and only vf19 reads it. At spawn (NPC vf146 0x3F0E8C -> 0x3F119C) the size is set first (0x4E3AD8 -> 0x7F8488 -> the setter); then come 0x3F08B0, the camera collision 0x44DC90, vf14 at 0x3F126E and vf111 0x41B6B4, which is why every spawned capsule matches its size. The player's slot 143 is 0x19BCE98. The player reaches the same core 0x3F1548 at run time (free aim clearing its target, and a movement-record change 0x187F94C); for NPCs the only shipped caller of slot 14 is the spawn path. Driven on a live NPC (the cast shape released, then vf14), the swap resizes him in place. Read back on one body after five swaps between x1.08 and x4.91, at x4.91 the scale read 4.910 and the committed radius and half height 196.40 / 510.64 cm, with the cast shape and the controller's shape at 196.40. char+0x218C, which equals the half height at rest, followed it (510.64, then 171.60 at x1.65), while a control body read its reset value 10000.0.

AI spacing and attack reach

Character/Models.CapsuleRadius is not the body and takes no size: it is the AI spacing radius. The callers of 0x30CEC0 use it in 6 two-sided gaps, max(0, dist(a, b) - (ra + rb)) (0x18BF90, 0x18DAE8, 0x2E48F4, 0x2E49B8, 0x378CEC, 0x1A039E0), in a single one-sided distance dist - r (0x2E44D8) and in one cache, 0xF1314 into char+0x2DD0 (NPCs only). dist corrects the height through 0x18C06C, so the vertical part does follow size. The other direct reader, 0x158494, is a ranged aim offset, CapsuleRadius x scale x 0.5.

Class CapsuleRadius Body dimensions
Orc_Defender, OlogHai_Infantry 65 40, 104, 40
Orc_Infantry, Orc_Spearman 50 40, 104, 40
Orc_Berserker, Orc_Archer 30 40, 104, 40
PlayerSkin_DarkTalion_ShadowWar 30 40, 104, 40 (CanScale false)

The damage test 0x2C658C passes an attack when min <= gap <= max (plus min(100, TranslationScaling. MaximumDistance) when ApplyDamageRangeIncludeTS is set) with gap = 0x18BF90(attacker, target), then the angle tests (ApplyDamageMaxHalfAngle). StrikeRange is a {min, max} copied into the translation-scaling state by 0x4E5970, which also compares the gap with InitialMaximumDistance. Over all 16,976 Combat/Attack records, 16,350 have a positive ApplyDamageRange maximum, with a median of 200 cm (10,599 exactly 200), a 10th percentile of 125 and a 90th of 1,000. At body contact the gap is 40 x (S + 1) - (r + 30) against Talion for an attacker of scale S and radius r, so a larger attacker's own CapsuleRadius makes the gap smaller.

On the Army screen the summary's size is +0x124 (filled by 0x169168; an unidentified captain gets Faction/Global.UnknownFactionMemberScale instead), read through 0x576DCC. 0x42F930(stage character, size) stores +0xB38, runs 0x402810, rescales a mount that uses the rider's scale and refreshes the attachments. 0x7C0F78(states, scale) takes the summary's size when the scale is 0 or less, stores it at +0x570 and raises bit 5 of +0x574 (applied by 0x37B260 -> 0x42EED0). 0x1C7500 compares +0x124 between summaries.

The player's movement record and speed

The movement object mv = [player+0x2F88] holds the live copy of Client/PlayerMovement. The copier 0x187F94C(mv, record) (callers 0x187F71C and 0x19BF068; re-run by Set / ClearCharacterModelOverride and Set / ClearFactionMember) writes +0x410 the character, +0x548 the record (it skips an unchanged one), +0x550 BaseSpeedMultiplier (1.0), +0x554 PlayerYawInterpolateTime (0.4) and +0x558 FallMinHeight (100.0). The character's own +0x550 reads 0. Writing +0x550 does not change the running speed: at 0.6 the top controller speed (the proxy's +0xB0) read 8.77 against 9.21 m/s and 8.08 against 8.53 m/s on two clean pairs, 0.95x against the 0.60x requested. Root motion drives the player, and what scales his motion is his clock (SlowMo.PlayerTimeScale, see Combat).

Moving the player: the immediate teleport

0x19C04D4(player, &transform{position, rotation}) is a two-argument player wrapper with no caller in the shipped executable. Unless the player is spectating (0x171A78: +0x2F0C, or a state query) it calls the player's immediate teleport, his vtable slot 111 0x7BA55C(player, &pos, &rot, ground snap = 1, zero velocity = 0, reset the combat component = 1 (0x7BA7E4)). The snap casts a vertical ray from the target plus char+0x2BC0 x scale down 10,000 cm ([0x202E9EC]): a hit puts the feet on it, and a miss keeps the target height. Zeroing the velocity runs 0x41BB78 -> 0x17347C. Nothing else is touched: the character arrives in one frame (34-64 ms after the request, 0.0 cm off target in 11 of 11 moves; a 4 m move snapped 76.5 cm onto the ground in 48 ms, with no fade and no loading screen). An AI character's slot 111 is another function (0x41B6B4, which the NPC spawn path also calls). The snap only looks down from its start, so a target inside a wall or under a slope lands beyond the wall or stays inside the terrain.

The world ray test the snap casts is 0x124B6C = vt_0x1E6ABA8 vf67([base+0x26FEFD0]+8, &query, &result); it reports a hit when it returns 1:

Query offset Meaning Result offset Meaning
+0x00 from (x, y, z cm) +0x00 the hit point
+0x0C to +0x0C / +0x18 the ray (written on every cast)
+0x18 u32 0x10 +0x24 the surface normal (a wall's y 0, flat ground 0.98)
+0x1C u8 1 +0x30 the plane distance
+0x20 u32 0x3C +0x38 / +0x40 the body (zeroed on a miss) / a u32 key (0xFFFF kept on a miss)

It hits terrain, walls and overhangs and passes through characters (5 of 5 orcs between 1.4 and 13 m were not hit).

The player's model change and the body build

One body in, one out. The player's model change, vf136 0x19BF404, compares the old model [char+0x2B30] with the new one after the base vf136. When they differ, it requests the old model's bundle unloaded (RequestBundleUnload 0x19EB87C(manager, Character/Models.Bundle(old))) and takes that record out of the build set (0x4277FC on owner+0x8E8). It then requests the new one's bundle (RequestBundleLoad 0x4B6204), adds its record (0x1156BDC), raises the build flag and calls 0x19CBA4C. No memory test sits anywhere on that path: the game stays within a small graphics card's memory by never holding more than one extra body.

The build flag is the byte player+0x2D40 (+0x2D41 is the flag to fire OnModelConfigComplete, vf134). vf136 raises it (the word 0x101). One function lowers it: the gate 0x19C0BA8, run for each pending player by the tick 0x4C6050 of the manager at [base+0x2A86880] (list +0x120), and only when both of these hold:

  1. 0x19EAD34: every record of CharacterBundleMgr's build set (+0x80) passes IsBundleLoaded 0x1665B4 (see Resource streaming);
  2. 0x7B86B8([player+0x2348]): the 10 weapon holders ([vc+0x38 + i*0x28] -> +0x140 -> +0xC0 -> byte +0x68) and the 15 gear slots ([vc+0x1C8 + i*0x28] -> +0xC0 -> +0x68) all read ready; a missing link counts as ready.

The flag's leaf getter is 0x1172488; at rest it reads 0 with 11 requests tested and none unready (6 holders vt_0x1DB1F68, 5 slot objects vt_0x1FDABF8). Two consumers wait on it: the scripted player-model setup 0x4C5EFC (fade reason 6) and a quest state, 0x7DDECC (vt_0x1F9DFB8 vf2, which reschedules itself while the flag is up and otherwise moves to state 0xF). A mission's fade held while the flag was up and cleared in the same 500 ms sample in which it fell, and builds after an arrival took 12 and 14 s. A body build that cannot finish is therefore a black screen when it lands inside a load, a mission start or a scripted swap, and only a slow build anywhere else.

Bodies on other rigs. A body on another rig plays the player's clips by bone name: an Olog body drives 47 of its 89 bones from Talion's locomotion clip (the rig comparison is in Animation, "Rigs compared").

Nazgul bodies have no solid materials. Of 11,791 material copies decoded, every Nazgul body, helmet, robe and weapon material is wraith_solid (or wraith_solid_cine) in a translucent slot with the solid slot empty; a wraith move re-applies the compiled set (see Runtime material slots below). A Nazgul's cape is not a piece slot at all. It draws from a cloth render node (vt_0x1DAADB8, built by 0x1B27D4; [node+0x90] is the body piece's material object + 0xA0, and nothing in the piece points back at the node) through the material handle that the node captured, [node+0x118] (vt_0x1DBC860, +0x08 the resource). A cloth node changes material only through a new handle: the app object's vf103 0x58B064 makes one from a .mat path, and the cloth wrapper node - 0x10 takes it through vf51 0x58AE0C, queued on the render ring. Writing a resource into the old handle is refused while the render side holds it locked (0x1BE7C4). The node's render layer [node+0xB2] (1 opaque, 5 translucent, 7 UI) comes from its constructor (5 for a ghost cloth). The passes pick nodes by it every frame, and wrapper vf31 0x58ADC4 changes it; one world held 4,207 cloth nodes, 3,529 on layer 1, 640 on layer 5 and 34 on layer 7. Monolith's solid twin of the cape's vertex-cloth shader, gear_lod0_vertcloth_nocull.fxo, is listed by 1 of 183,631 shipped bundle manifests (Castamir's), and a shader tree loads only through a manifest that lists it (see Resource streaming).

Wraith World and the wraith companion

Wraith World is a weapon mode: Combat/PlayerWeaponModes/VisionMode -> Client/VisionMode::VM_Wraith. The call 0x6A6CE4 -> 0x6A7E90(vision = [player+0x2FA8], mode) drives the vision object, whose +0x28 is the mode record and +0x44 the active flag; an amount eases from +0x48 to the target +0x4C over the mode's 1 s ramp (ShaderVisionMode, read by 0x115EF98). The game changes nothing on the player's own pieces: Talion's solid shaders cut his body away with the same reveal map the wraith draws with (tRevealMapArray, k_fRevealValue). Orc and human shaders carry only k_fDissolve. A presentation sets Client/VisionMode::VM_Presentation on the vision object (+0x28). With the presentation exit below cleared, the mode stays active meanwhile (+0x44 stays 1).

The wraith mode's exits are data. Combat/PlayerWeaponModes/Modes::PC_WraithPing keeps four packed bools in bool word 0 of its instance, all true as shipped (0x0F): bit 0 ExitOnTakeDamage, bit 1 ExitOnCameraPresentationActive, bit 2 ExitOnStartAnyAttack and bit 3 ExitOnDamageEnemy. Each has an exclusion: the attack category Combat/Category::PC_DontDisable_WraithPing_Generic, and for presentations ExcludeExitOnPresentationEvent = Intro_WraithVisionTut, MemorableMoments_OrcVPlayer, MemorableMoments_Switch. OnDuration is 0 (no timeout), the vision mode is PingMode -> VM_Wraith and the material transform WraithPing. The included transition PC_Wraith_Vision_Cursed ships all four false. The exit check 0x590780 (its only caller is 0x59053C) leaves the mode:

  • on the timer;
  • on a hit taken, a hit on an enemy or an attack started, each unless the attack uses the exclusion category (CombatManager::DoesAttackUseCategory);
  • on a presentation not in the exclusion list (0x183D548);
  • on the predicate's CancelAttackCategory and on RequiredSimultaneousModes;
  • on a new mode that the record does not allow (0x183D5A4). The AllowedModes list has 30 entries, among them bow, zoom, sniper, shotgun, stealth, slow motion, the Radiance area attacks and the fell beast.

The four switches are read live by that function alone, with no copy, so a cleared bit applies at the next attack. The mode's predicate Combat/PlayerWeaponModes/Predicate::PC_WraithPing is a toggle on CombatSecondary01 with no presentation condition. It sets IsToggle and UseRawInput, requires the item CP_WraithVision and a player who is neither poisoned nor cursed, and is canceled by the category PC_Disable_WraithPing_Generic.

The wraith form is the player's companion model (Combat/CompanionModel), a copy of the player built from a SimpleModel (CombatCompanionModelInstance vt_0x1DBD198). Its manager, vt_0x1DCC8F8 at [[player+0x26A8]+0x80], holds the body instance at +0x38, the head instance at +0x60 and the companion record at +0x68. Wraith moves add echoes that clone it: Combat/Echo::QuickThrow is three PC_Echo with CopyPlayerCompanion and the material set Echo. Monolith's wraith pieces carry a default set of translucent wraith materials (wraith_game.fxo, fed the character's own textures) plus depth proxies (wraith_proxy.fxo) and an Echo set (wraith_riddick.fxo).

Material sets are compiled. 0x1B6190C builds a cache at start-up at [GM+0x6DD0]+0x18: one entry per Model record of 0x48-byte compiled sets. Editing a record later changes nothing there, and asking for a set index past an entry's count returns null.

Visuals, pieces and weapons

A visual (vt_0x1DA9610) has a spec at [visual+0xB8] (23 piece slots at +0x28) and a skeleton at [visual+0x90]. SetPieceSlot 0x4C62EC(spec, slot, piece, armor design, visual) swaps one slot live (the engine's live armor equip). A SimpleModel builder (0x4C5194) sets body, head, hair, accessories, flair and helmet from its entry; piece slots 8-12 come only from items. Pieces follow the host's pose through 0xF5A290 (pieces at skel+0x108 into the host's pose list). Visibility 0x1BEE8C (skeleton, flag) is the engine's "hide the base model", and the material controller's HideBaseModel transform calls exactly that. 0x58BCF0 sets the material-set type.

Weapons are drawn by per-slot holders [vc + 0x38 + slot * 0x28] (vt_0x1DB1F68; a shield carrier's slot 0 holder is vt_0x1DCA710 with the same layout: +0x140 the item, +0x148 / +0x150 the built elements, +0x1F1 shown). An element is an object reference (vt_0x1DB4F58: +0x08 next, +0x10 previous, +0x18 itself, +0x20 the target). Its target is the built object (vt_0x1F782C0), whose +0x120 is the weapon's model (vt_0x1DB4948, null while not drawn). The model holds +0xE0 the wielder, +0x120 the material object, +0x170 the mesh path and, at +0xC0, an embedded reference link (vt_0x1DAA9D0). This chain found 54 of 55 models on 27 Army-stage characters and every weapon of 16 of 16 world characters (the 55th model was a warg mount's). A weapon skeleton has three bones (root, handle, tip). 0x3FD228 builds only an empty holder. An equipped, drawn weapon is rebuilt by the engine's refresh 0x45ABE4 per holder: 0x3FD21C (hide, which destroys every model), then 0x3FD204 (show, which builds from the item as it is now). The equip setup (vf35 0x3FCF70) runs the same pair. The weapon's look chain is Inventory/EquippedWeaponData.Model -> _StructuresInventoryItemModel.Models -> _StructuresInventoryModel.ModelRecord -> a Model -> its default material set. Equipped weapon slots are vectors at vc+0x4D0 + i * 0x18 (0 sword, 1 dagger, 2 bow). An item's +0xA9 is "equipped", +0x40 its equipped-weapon data and +0x2C its holder index. The weapon type comes from the item's Inventory/Items.WeaponType, copied into the built weapon object (+0x188, by 0x3FCF70); for the active weapon the chain is [char+0x2348] -> slot [vc+0x728] -> [vc + idx * 0x28 + 0x38] -> +0x188.

Runtime material slots. A piece's material object (piece+0x48, vt_0x1FFE3E8) has one table of 48 slots of 0x18 bytes from +0x1E8: slots 0-15 solid, 16-31 translucent (+0x368) and 32-47 depth proxy (+0x4E8). A slot's first field is the .mat resource. The setter is the object's vtable slot 47, 0x17AF0C(object, kind, index, resource), where kind 0 is solid, 1 translucent and 2 depth proxy. It references the resource and posts to the render command ring, so it belongs on the main thread. SetMaterialSet 0x4DC938(skeleton, type) re-applies a set's solid entries.

Render IDs are set by SetRenderID 0x1BF144(visual, id), through the skeleton's vf4 0x1BF3AC, onto body, head, pieces and attachments. The player has 255, his wraith copy 254, a player-aligned mount 212, followers 210, enemies 220 and the Army stage's captains 245. 0x1BF3AC(model, u8 id) stores model+0x40, then gives the id to the base piece [model+0x100] and the 23 slots [model+0x108 + i*8] through 0x1BF49C, to [model+0xE0] through 0x1BF96C, to [model+0xD0] through 0x58D648, and to each child in [[model+0xC8]+0x138 .. +0x140] through 0x1BF388. 0x1BF49C calls the piece's render instance ([piece+0x48] vf36, 0x1BF4DC), which queues a render command (ring 0x272E8D8, 0x114D4E4, callback 0x677070), then passes the id to the piece's child [piece+0x58]: each piece can carry its own id, applied by the render thread a frame later. The per-pixel id is the alpha byte of the G-buffer target with id 7 (RGBA8; a float copy sits in the alpha of target 19), so the engine's own raster says which character, or which piece, covers a pixel. One frame of the Army screen held four values: 245 for the stage captains, 1 for the sky and background, 0, and 220 for an enemy (see Rendering). In-world characters stay in material-set type Wraith permanently (the type's override is the pale vision glow). No shipped Model carries a Wraith-typed set; the set types in use are default 2,644, GhostWeapon 133, Echo 78, HierarchyUnknown 65, PlayerCompanion 17 and Death 11.

Mounts. The ridden mount is [player+0x2578]; its rider is [mount+0x25A0] and its visual [mount+0x2340]. A mount has no material controller (its combat object's +0x390 is 0).

Talion's material controller

The controller is ctrl = [[player+0x26A8]+0x390], an object with no vtable. It animates the shader parameters of Talion and of his wraith double; no other character has one (0 of 43 spawned characters).

Offset Meaning

+0x30 owner

A parameter entry holds +0x00 the parameter name's hash, +0x04 the priority of its driving state, +0x08 an easing object (only while easing), +0x10 / +0x14 start and end time, +0x18 the transform driving it, +0x20 default, +0x28 current, +0x30 from and +0x38 to. On the Dark Talion skin the self block holds 8 entries (k_fRevealValue, k_fCurse, k_fRevealArm, k_fDepthAdjust, k_nRevealMapIndex and three colors) and the child block 11 (k_fFresnelGlow 0.5, k_fRevealArm 0, k_fRevealWidth 0.1, k_fFixedReveal_Active 0, k_fOpacityMin 0, k_fDepthAdjust 0.25, k_fOpacity 0, k_nRevealMapIndex 0 and three colors).

  • SetSetup 0x739BC0(ctrl, setup) builds the entries (0x73A5E8 takes the RestoreDefaultTransform value if a transform names the parameter, otherwise its initial value) and returns immediately when the setup is already current, so the setup record is read only when it changes.
  • The update 0x58B488 -> 0x58B850 eases entries (current = lerp(from, to)) and pushes them; an idle entry pushes nothing.
  • AddState 0x58E320(ctrl, state, ...) is refused when the state's tag is in the setup's StateTagRestrictions (0x58BAFC). An entry follows a state only when its priority is at least the current driver's. RemoveState 0x58B370 removes every node of a record, and each parameter it drove eases to the next remaining state's value, or to its default. A state that drives a parameter the setup does not list creates an entry whose default is that state's value. The script bindings are CPlayerObj::AddMaterialState 0x19BB020 / RemMaterialState 0x19BEF8C.
  • After every equip the engine re-attaches the blocks (0x7A7738 attaches the self block to the player and the held weapon, and the child block to the double), because a piece added later is set up by 0x4C6EFC without them. The child block reaches only the double's twin of a weapon ([[model+0x20]+0x128]). A held weapon gets the self block alone, so a wraith-shader weapon's own .mat opacity decides whether it shows outside Wraith World.

The handles. A block's handle is bound into every piece of its skeleton, so a parameter set on the handle reaches every shader on those pieces that declares its name. Talion's block reaches his solid armor (talion_armor.fxo, 59 parameters: tint zones and their curse twins, blood color, value and alpha, k_fDissolve, k_fOpacity, specular tints) and the wraith-shader pieces he holds. The double's block reaches wraith_game*.fxo (52 parameters, among them k_fBloomScale, k_fFresnelMin / Max, k_fAlphaFloor, k_fDesaturate, k_fLightIntensity, k_fAmbient). The controller pushes only its own entries, so a value set on the handle directly lasts until the handle is replaced (by a skin change or a reload). The handle has no remove slot.

The 168 material states are parameter states; none hides a model or swaps cloth. WraithRadiance, Vanish, WraithPing, Domination, Tower and GameChanger set Talion's k_fRevealValue to 0 and the double's k_fRevealArm to 1 and k_fOpacity to 1, which cuts the body away and shows the double. TalionDeathSwitch shows both, HandOfGod and Stealth draw a fainter double, and _Curse sets k_fCurse to 1. Player_Eltariel, TalionWraith and DarkTalion are color sets. A body whose tree keeps the setup Player_DarkTalion but has no companion double is cut away by those states and shows nothing in their place. The setup Combat/FX/MaterialController/Setup::Baranor restricts the Talion and Celebrimbor states and pushes no reveal.

The wraith shader (wraith_game.fxo) is a skeletal pixel shader. Its material constant buffer cb3 follows the .mat parameter order: cb3[9] holds the fixed-reveal width and active flag and the reveal width and arm; cb3[10] the fixed-reveal arm, reveal map index, dissolve and fresnel minimum; cb3[11] the fresnel maximum, alpha floor, opacity and opacity minimum; and cb3[12].x the bloom scale. Reveal = saturate((sample + (W + 1) x Arm - 1) / W) x (1 - Dissolve), with W the reveal width, and alpha is roughly fresnel x reveal x k_vObjectColor.w x opacity x diffuse alpha. k_vObjectColor is a global shader-constant object (0x2A39FA0, value at +0x40) filled per draw (0x254B80) from a packed color at draw item +0x4C.

Sauron's two forms

The final boss is two combat trees, Combat/Tree::AI_Sauron_WarForm and AI_Sauron_FairForm. Walking the RecordLinks from each tree, the war form reaches 116 attacks, 37 of them his own (all 16 mace swings among them), and the fair form 187, 52 of them his own. In the shipped data the fair ranged chain (AI_Sauron_Fair_Ranged_Auto -> _2 -> _3 -> _Last), AI_Sauron_Fair_Melee, AI_Sauron_Fair_To_Warform and AI_Sauron_Curse_AoE have no referrer (a reverse scan of 694,632 records and 2,679 world scripts; nodes built at run time were not checked). His 24 counter syncs (SRNF_CB_Sync_*) pair with Celebrimbor's halves on the player rig; no orc half ships. The fair and player rigs bind every body bone at 0.0 cm (only the finger curls differ), and the fair body's static mesh has nothing between the belt and the boots.

His effects are keyed. His moves play 45 distinct MeleeFX: 34 resolve through lookup tables whose only drawing entry is Sauron, 7 name an effect directly, 3 are Nazgul-only and one (Sauron_PushBack) names nothing and draws nothing. Of the 53 decoded effects, 29 carry a Sound node with his own audio hook, so his combat sounds live inside those keyed effects. His trees also carry the war HitFXSetup Sauron_WarForm, the fair HitFXSetup BlackHand and StateFX Sauron_FairForm, AttackTypeFX Nazgul on both and the war form's PersistentClientFX sauron_wf_persistent.fxp.

FX lookup. The resolver is 0x669038(table, mode, source key, target key): mode 0 takes the source's key, 1 the target's, 2 the player's ([[[CGameClientShell]+0x888]+0x26A8]+0x68), with no fallback ("FAILED to find a Valid Key, FX will not play"). The compiled cache is [GameManager+0x6C70] {data, count +0x08, key category +0x10}, with data[key index] = {key, per-key object}. A per-key object is {+0x00 data, +0x08 count, +0x10 table category}, with data[table index] = {table, entry}; entry 0 means that the key draws nothing for that table. 0x66916C reads _StructuresFXLookupTableEntry.FX. A record's index is (rec - [[rec+0x18]+0x38]) / 0x28. Of 619 live lookup tables (620 shipped), 54 have a Sauron entry, 243 a DarkTalion entry and 261 a Talion entry, and 264 have a Talion or Dark Talion entry but no Sauron one, while 0 of 620 carry both a Sauron and a DarkTalion entry.

His teleport. Eight recoils (AI_Sauron_Recoil_Teleport_F / B / L / R and the _RMT_ set) and four ranged dodges play Naz_SaurFair_TeleportAway (in nazgul_prototype.anix, 566 ms), whose events are STARTFACING at 0, BEFACING at 200, COMBATTELEPORTTOOBJECT at 300, and EAL and EnableRecoilOnStop at 533 ms. Every one of these moves requires a target. Its MeleeFX Sauron_Teleport (keyed to Sauron) is sauron_teleport_out_anim.fxp, 2.8 s long: a fire-smoke burst on Torso_Base at 0.16 - 0.22 s, a light, a shock ring, embers and smoke that follow him, and no sound. His recoil cooldown AI_Sauron_Fair_TeleportRecoil is 8 s. His teleport sound cbt_srnf_tele_out is posted only by SRNF_TransToFair_Recoil at 33 ms. Birds and other flying creatures

Crows, bats, seagulls and the desert raptors are not characters. Each is an AvianObject (vt_0x1DBBD30, 0x518 bytes, derived from the generic game object, without a character controller, behavior graph or inventory) spawned by a placed world object, an AvianMarker (vt_0x1FA4CD0). A marker owns at most one bird.

Definitions

Avian/Definition holds 14 species records: Crow, Crow_Eat, bat, bat_Day, bat_DaySleep, bat_AnyTime, Seagul, Seagull_Eat, raptor_Desert, raptor_Desert_Day, raptor_Desert_Night, raptor_Desert_Night_Clear, testing and testingBenchmark. A definition names the model (Model.Cha_Crow, Cha_Bat, Cha_Seagull, Cha_RaptorDesert), a scale range (crows 1.0-1.5, the others 0.9-1.1), a weather / time-of-day predicate (for example Weather/Predicates/Forecast.AnyTimeNoRain, NightNoRain or DayNoWeather), a combat tree (Hunting_Crow, Hunting_Bat, Hunting_Seagull, Hunting_Avian), a render-ID setup and seven animation slots, each with a weighted variant list (perched, eating, take-off, bank left, bank right, flap, landing). It also holds perch and flight durations (average and variance), take-off height and distance, climb, descent, turn and lateral rates, a death effect, a respawn time and the group-sound settings. A crow perches for 9 +/- 6 s and flies for 60 +/- 11 s, bats fly for 900 s, and every species respawns after 33-120 s.

+0x1DD / +0x1DE activated / the spawn roll passed

Per marker the sweep calls 0x43AE38 with the player's position. A disabled marker, or one whose definition's weather predicate fails against [GameManager+0x6D98], loses its bird. Distances are horizontal (X and Z) and measured to the player only. On entering the activation radius, the marker rolls once: random(0, 99.9) < ActivationPercent. On success it spawns a bird (0x1A41FFC) with the definition's model and a scale drawn from the scale range and stored at the bird's +0xC8; the bird is of class AvianObject and is created through the object server. While activated, a marker with no bird respawns once the engine clock passes +0x1D8. Beyond the deactivation radius the bird is removed and the out-of-range script event fires. When a bird is destroyed, the marker sets +0x1D8 = now + RespawnTime x 1000 (0x575ED4; 5 s when the field is absent). A respawn time of 0 or less clears the roll instead, so nothing returns until the player leaves and comes back.

In a live desert region, 301 markers (raptor_Desert_Day 157, raptor_Desert 63, raptor_Desert_Night 57, raptor_Desert_Night_Clear 24) used activation radii of 120-130 m, a deactivation radius of 140 m, fear distances of 30-40 m and activation chances of 25, 38 and 40 percent, and 250 were AlwaysFlying. Of 28 markers whose radius covered the player, 21 were activated and 6 had passed the roll, giving 6 birds. Each was linked both ways (marker+0x1D0 and bird+0x390) and flew 7-26 m from its marker at 0.5-6 m above it, with scales of 0.92-1.07.

The update (vf18, 0xEF820) advances the state machine at +0x410:

State Function Behavior
0 perched 0x1A5B6B8 perched (or eating) animations; leaves after the perch time or when the player comes within the fear distance of the marker
1 take-off 0x1A5B8A0 take-off animation and climb from the take-off height and distance
2 flying 0xEFC48, steering 0xEFD10 flies for the flight time around the marker or its orbit center above FlightBottom; turns pick the bank animations, straight flight the flap animations (threshold pi/16); a FlyOff marker's bird is destroyed
3 descending 0x1A5B3E8 landing animation down to the landing height; fear sends it back to 2
4 landing 0x1A5B508 touchdown, then 0 (or 2 on fear)
5 attacking 0x1A5B390 plays a pending attack

AvianMarker.Script_StartAttack (0x1A41FBC -> 0x1A5B160) picks an attack from the bird's combat tree for a target and enters state 5. The four bird trees (Hunting_Crow, _Avian, _Bat, _Seagull) have no attack nodes in a running game's merged database (132 of 281 combat trees do), so the call finds no attack.

Killing a bird. A hit goes through the hittable interface's slot 8 (0x1A5AA58), which runs the same damage evaluation as for characters (0x2E3B6C) and kills only when the damage is greater than 0. Death (0x1A5B01C) sets +0x510, fires the marker's AvianKilledScript event, plays the definition's death effect, posts combat event 0xB and destroys the object; the hit routine also calls the hit-counter increment 0x2DC668. The ranged targeting predicate WraithRanged_Targeting lists all six Hunting_* character types (avian, bat, crow, rat, seagull, spider).

Group sounds. 0x441A3C counts the birds of each definition within GroupSoundDistance, perched and in flight separately, and picks the none / low / high loop from the LowCount / HighCount thresholds.
