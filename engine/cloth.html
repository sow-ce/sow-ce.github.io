Cloth: GPU cloth and Havok Cloth

The game's two cloth systems: the engine's own GPU cloth, a position-based solver in a compute shader fed by CRES files, with the CRES container and its five kinds, the cloth file's six buffers, how a skeleton or an effect names its cloth, the solver step by step with its constants, and how simulated cloth is drawn; and the Havok Cloth carried inside skeletons.

The game ships two cloth systems. The engine's own GPU cloth simulates orc and olog gear, a few player and Eltariel bodies and cloaks, an Easterling cape, banners, flags, warg drool and a weapon's cloth in a compute shader, from <stem>.gpucloth.cres files; Havok Cloth, carried inside skeleton files, drives the player's capes. A piece uses one or the other: none of the 132 skeleton sections that name a GPU cloth also carries Havok cloth.

The CRES container

A .cres file is a container of GPU buffers. One reader loads every kind (0x72E4A8, slot 3 of vt_0x230A868; resource type 12):

u32 'CRES' | u32 version (1 - 5)
version >= 3:  u32 tag | u32 tag version          the pair must be one of the table below
string:        u16 length + bytes                 read and dropped (at most 259)
version >= 4:  u32 n + n bytes                    the resource's own blob
version >= 3:  u32 buffer count (else 1)
per buffer     u32 count | u32 stride | u32 format  then count x stride bytes

In version 5 the format word is the StringHash of a format name, looked up in a table the reader builds at 0x27AF880: Float32, UInt32, SInt32, UInt16x2, UInt8, UInt8x4, UNorm8, UNorm8x4, FloatR11G11B10, Float16x2, UNorm16x4, Float16x4, Float32x2, Float32x4 (typed buffers, ids 0 - 13) and Structured (a structured buffer of the given stride). A name outside the table refuses the file. Version 2 - 4 store the id directly; version 1 stores only a byte size of 32-bit words. The reader also accepts a CRES wrapped in an SLVT version 4 envelope. The loaded object is 0x48 bytes (constructor 0x72EC50): the blob at +0x00, the buffer pointers at +0x10, their byte sizes at +0x28.

Tag Tag version Shipped Buffers Files
GPUC 2 240 in bundles + 12 loose 6 (below) + blob <stem>.gpucloth.cres, the cloth file
PRTS 4 1,322 + 41 4 structured (strides 16, 12, 8, 16) + blob <model>_ps.cres, prefab _fp.cres
LOCT 1 122 + 115 1 UInt32 <map>.lsomap.cres and .env.cres (loader 0x1CBD2D8)
SSTR 1 39 + 63 1 UInt32 + blob world layers' .osc_*.cres, named by an SSTFile property (loader 0x1B50440)
HAIR 3 1 loose 1 structured (stride 76) materials\0dev\engine\hairlookup_array.cres

Every shipped CRES is version 5 with exactly the tag version in the table, and its name string is empty. The 827 distinct payloads parse to their last byte and re-emit byte for byte. A sweep of the path tables of all 190,407 bundle containers found 1,723 .cres sections in 482 containers; each carries the CRES magic, and none of the other 146,778 sections in those containers does.

The GPU cloth file (GPUC)

The blob is u8 transform count, u8 capsule count, u8 bone[transform count]. The six buffers come in a fixed order, and the cloth dispatcher binds them to the compute shader's resources in that order:

# Shader resource Format Record Meaning
0 k_ClothConstraintsBuffer (t0) Structured, 20 u32 i, u32 j, f32 wi, f32 wj, f32 rest distance constraints
1 k_ClothBendSpringsBuffer (t1) Structured, 32 4 x {u32 partner, f32 rest} bend springs
2 k_ClothNodeDataBuffer (t3) Structured, 24 u32 n0, n1, n2, n3, f32 inverse mass, i32 bone slot particles
3 k_ClothSpringGroupsBuffer (t2) UInt32 8 x u32 constraint batch sizes
4 k_ClothResetBuffer (t4) Structured, 12 f32 x3 rest positions
5 k_ClothBoundingBuffer (t5) Structured, 36 u32 bone slot, f32 A[3], f32 D[3], f32 1/|D|², f32 1/r² collision capsules

What the 118 distinct shipped cloth files hold:

  • Particles. At most 254 per file, one per thread of a 256-thread group. A particle with inverse mass 0 is pinned to its bone slot, and every other particle has slot -1: 5,120 pinned and 13,275 free particles out of 18,395, with no exception. A slot indexes the blob's bone list, which uses the same bone numbers as the mesh's skinning palette. The blob holds at most 16 bones.
  • The frame stencil. n0 - n3 are not constraints. The solver rebuilds each particle's normal as normalize(a × c + c × b + b × d + d × a), where a - d are the vectors to n0, n1, n2 and n3 (the fan runs n0, n2, n1, n3). The tangent is normalize(P[n0] − P[n1]). A slot that names the particle itself contributes nothing, which is how a particle with fewer neighbors is padded (10,409 of 73,580 slots).
  • Constraints. On all 44,937 edges, wi = invMass_i / (invMass_i + invMass_j), wi + wj = 1, and rest equals the exact rest distance. No edge joins two pinned particles.
  • Batches. The eight counts add up to the edge count, and no particle appears twice inside one batch (118 of 118 files), so each batch is solved one edge per thread with no write conflict. Batches 0 - 3 hold the structural edges and 4 - 7 the shear edges: none of the 23,680 edges in batches 0 - 3 is a quad diagonal, against 17,524 of the 21,257 in batches 4 - 7. At most 107 edges sit in one batch.
  • Bend springs. Each of the four rest distances is the straight rest distance to the partner. Of the 73,192 used slots, 40,544 point two edges away and 9,588 further; 23,060 name the particle itself and do nothing.
  • Capsules. The segment runs from A to A + D in the bind space of its bone. +0x1C is 1/|D|² (354 of 354) and the radius is 1/√(+0x20), from 7.2 to 66 cm (median 15.6). There are at most 12 per file.
  • The mesh. The particles are one draw group of the mesh with the same stem, in the same bundle: every one of 117 files has a group with exactly as many vertices as particles, and particle i sits at vertex i. The mesh stores half-precision positions and the cloth file the full-precision copy: 113 files agree within half a half-float step and the others within 0.015 cm. A pinned particle's bone is always one of its vertex's skin bones (5,052 of 5,052), and in 4,924 cases the heaviest one.

How a cloth file is found

Only two places in the code request a CRES resource.

The skeleton names it. The skeleton loader (0x504420) reads, after the per-bone bytes, a Havok cloth block, then a GPU cloth list:

0xDCDCFF04  Havok cloth (below)
0xDCDCFF05  u32 count
            count x { u32 0xBEEFFEED | u16 length + path (requested as a CRES)
                      f32, f32, f32, f32, f32, u32, f32, f32, u32, f32 }
0xDEADDEAD
0xDCDCFF99  skeleton physics

The ten values have the order and types of the GPUCloth effect node's creation record below: density, stretch, shear and bend stiffness, dissipation, iteration count, full and reduced simulation distance, shared group and wind factor. The entries are kept 0x30 bytes apart from skeleton +0x30. All 24,378 skeleton sections in the bundles carry the block, and 132 of them (93 distinct skeletons: 70 orc, 17 olog, 3 player, 2 Eltariel, 1 Easterling) list one cloth each. Every listed path has the skeleton's own stem (132 of 132) and is a shipped cloth file (93 of 93). Across those entries the iteration count is 2 in 120 of 132 (at most 12), the two distances are always 100 and 1000, the shared group is always 0, and the wind factor is 1.0 (130) or 0.5 (2).

The GPUCloth effect node. ClientFX.GPUCloth is a Model node (key vt_0x200B358 on the Model key vt_0x1DC8910) with twelve properties of its own, stored in the key by its property loader (0x1D549B8):

Key offset Property Key offset Property
+0x148 Density +0x160 FullSimDist
+0x14C StretchStiffness +0x164 ReducedSimDist
+0x150 ShearStiffness +0x168 SharedGroup (int)
+0x154 BendStiffness +0x16C WindFactor
+0x158 Dissipation +0x170 ApplyLocalWindSources (bool)
+0x15C NumIterations (int) +0x171 ApplyWindDamping (bool)

The key's second slot (0x1D54ADC) takes the node's model path, drops its extension, appends .GPUCloth.cres and stores the resource handle at +0x140. When the effect starts (0x64FE94) it builds the model, then passes the handle and the ten values from Density to WindFactor to the cloth system; the two wind switches are not part of that record. The 25 cloth files that no skeleton names are a weapon's cloth, two warg drool files and 22 banners, flags and hanging clothes in the world.

Character switches. Model.EnableCloth (read by 0x111948) is applied by 0x1118E4 to the model's object at +0xD0 and to the object at +0x58 of each of the 23 piece slots that start at +0x108. Faction/World/Character.ApplyGPUClothTranslations (read by 0x7C439C), when false, makes 0x1B464C0 clear byte +0x28 of the object at +0xD8 and of the object at +0x60 of each piece slot.

The solver

The compute shader is shaders\shadertrees\compute\cloth_physics.fxo. The renderer loads it through the materials clothphysics.mat and clothphysicsreset.mat (0x1CA5674, fields +0x808 and +0x820 of the static engine-material table at 0x2A37740). The tree holds two cs_5_0 programs, fulldeferred_rigid and translucent_rigid, and the reset tree one.

Dispatch. The dispatcher (0x101A9C0) walks the live cloth instances and groups consecutive instances of the same cloth resource, up to eight. For each group 0x10199C0 maps the bone block, fills each instance (0x101B440) and dispatches one 256-thread group per instance. The instances of a group share the six buffers and the counts. Each has its own 60-byte particle state buffer (k_RwClothPosBuffer0 - 7, from instance +0x30) and its own constants.

State offset Field
+0x00 position
+0x0C velocity
+0x18 normal
+0x24 tangent
+0x30 position at the start of the step

The reset program sets position and previous position to the rest position and velocity to zero, and builds the frame from the stencil.

One step, one thread per particle, positions held in group-shared memory:

  1. A pinned particle takes P = R(q) · rest + t from its bone. k_mModelObjectNodes holds, per bone, a translation and then a rotation quaternion (32 bytes), indexed by transform count × instance + slot; the dispatcher maps transform count × instance count × 32 bytes of it.
  2. A free particle integrates F = bend + (wind − v) · |(wind − v) · n| · invMass · Params1.w + Force, then v′ = (v + F · dt) · damping and P′ = P + v′ · dt. The bend term pushes a particle away from each spring partner that is closer than its rest distance: F −= dir · max(Params1.z · (rest − length), 0).
  3. Two constraint phases sweep the eight batches. The first runs Params3.x iterations when Params3.y ≥ 1e-4, the second Params2.x iterations when Params3.w ≥ 1e-4. Each edge's length is clamped into [rest, rest · k], with k = Params1.x for batches 0 - 3 and Params1.y for batches 4 - 7, and the correction is shared by wi and wj. After each sweep every particle is pushed out of each capsule: the closest point on the segment is Q = A + D · saturate((P − A) · D / |D|²), and when |P − Q|² / r² ≤ 1 the particle moves to Q + (P − Q) · r / |P − Q|. A capsule's A and D are carried by its bone's transform first.
  4. The normal and tangent are rebuilt from the stencil. Velocity becomes (P′ − P) · Params2.w, which is 1/dt, unless that constant is 0.

The translucent_rigid program first carries every particle's position, velocity and normal through the instance's k_mClothMoveTransform (three rows), the owner's move since the last step. 0x101B440 fills it from instance +0x54 - +0x90 when bit 0 of instance +0x48 is set, and with the identity otherwise. fulldeferred_rigid has no such step.

Constant Object cb0 rows Contents (per instance)
k_vClothWind 0x273A910 0 - 7 air velocity
k_vClothForce 0x273A9D0 8 - 15 acceleration
k_mClothMoveTransform 0x273AA90 16 - 39 three rows each
k_vClothSimulationParams1 0x273AC50 40 - 47 structural limit, shear limit, bend stiffness, wind drag

Each array sits at its constant object +0x38, and the resource of a buffer constant at +0x40. In 0x101B440, Params1 = (instance +0xD4, +0xD8, +0xDC / +0x90, +0xD0) and Params2 = (+0xA0, saturate(1 − dt · +0xE0), dt from +0x98, 1/dt). When no step is pending (instance +0xA0 and +0xA8 both zero), dt and 1/dt are written as 0. The transform and capsule counts come from the cloth file's first two blob bytes.

Drawing simulated cloth

The cloth materials use the gpu_cloth shader trees (solid\gpu_cloth\gpuclothspecalphatestnocul.fxo, gpuclothspecalphatestnocultintsever.fxo) or the wraith *_gpucloth trees. Every vertex program of the first (15 of 15) reads k_ClothPosBuffer, a 48-byte structured buffer indexed by the vertex id, holding position, velocity, normal and tangent. Texture coordinates and color still come from the mesh. The previous-frame position for motion vectors is position − velocity · cb0[26].z, and k_ClothInvTransform1 - 3 map the result back.

Havok Cloth

Havok Cloth data sits in the skeleton's 0xDCDCFF04 block as Havok tagfiles (hclClothData, hclSimClothData, skin and mesh-deform operators, capsule colliders, bone-plane constraints); its setup classes carry the 2015.1 suffix (hctClothSetup20151Options). Of the 24,378 skeleton sections, 153 carry some. It works in meters, and it reads the wearer's bones through the same piece-to-rig table as the mesh. The Cloth.* console variables (MaxTimeStep, SettleNumSteps, RunAsTask, ScaleUpBoneCloth and others) and the hcl*Task classes belong to it, not to the GPU cloth.

Open questions

  • How StretchStiffness, ShearStiffness, BendStiffness, Dissipation and Density become instance +0x90 - +0xE0: shipped stretch values are mostly 0.01, while the edge clamp needs Params1.x ≥ 1, which suggests 1 + stretch. The cloth system's creation function would settle it.
  • Which of the two solver programs a dispatch uses, and what sets the move-transform bit.
  • How the 60-byte solver state becomes the 48-byte buffer the vertex programs read.
  • k_mModelObjectNodes declares 128 float4s (64 bones). A group of more than four instances of a 16-bone cloth would need more; whether the dispatcher limits groups by bone count is unread.
  • How the world's banners and flags reach the loader, since no skeleton names them.
  • What the PRTS, LOCT, SSTR and HAIR buffers mean; only their shapes and paths are known.
  • Which of the per-piece objects at +0x58 and +0x60 is the GPU cloth instance.
