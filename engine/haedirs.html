Haedirs: towers, seeing stones and fast travel

The campaign's 15 Haedir towers: the three pieces of saved state a purified tower is made of, the seeing-stone store and how the engine derives the tower items from it, the purify path, the achievement, the region challenge and the story gate, and how a tower's flame and eye decide their color.

The campaign has 15 Haedirs, three each in Minas Ithil, Cirith Ungol, Nurnen, Gorgoroth and Seregost. Purifying one at its seeing stone unlocks fast travel to it, lights its flame and counts toward the region's challenge. The engine keeps a purified Haedir as three separate pieces of saved state, and derives one of them from another when the player enters the world.

A purified Haedir is three pieces of state

Piece Where Read by
the seeing stone's owned byte the stone store [GM+0x6D28] the stone at its initialization (its flame), and the item reconcile when the player enters the world
the item <Region>_Tower_0N the player's inventory fast travel, the map marker, region progress, DLC side quests, the tower's own script
GameData.<Region>Tower_0N the GameData variable store no GameDB record reads it (its 15 predicates have no users)

(The Minas Ithil variables are spelled MinasItihlTower_* in the data.)

The seeing-stone store

[GM+0x6D28] keeps the stones in the same layout as the GameData store (GM+0x6C58) and the quest store (GM+0x6C60): an array of {record, u8 owned} entries of 0x10 bytes at +0x70, the u32 count at +0x78, and at +0x80 the category it holds, WorldPuzzle/SeeingStone (16 records: the 15 stones and _Base).

Function What it does
get 0x19C90CC(store, record) asserts "Bad record index" unless the record's category is the store's, indexes by RecordIndexOf 0x1898E8, checks that the entry holds the same record, returns the byte != 0 (0x19C91A0: is any stone owned)
set 0x19C9BA4(store, stone object) finds the entry for the stone's record ([stone+0x1B0]) through 0x19C9014 and writes 1; no event, no other flag; its only caller is the purify
load 0x19C94D0(table, stream) a count, then per entry a record (by hash, 0x17A9528) and the byte for that record: the store is part of the save

The stone object is a SeeingStone (vt_0x200B5E8). It keeps its record at +0x1B0 and a copy of the owned byte at +0x211, taken by its initialization (vf16 0x1D60140; the copy is made at 0x1D601D9). The script method SeeingStone:IsPlayerOwned (0x1170DF4) returns that cached byte. The script class has no setter: its members are Cast, Create, GetCanExit, GetEnabled, IsPlayerOwned, Serialize, SetCanExit and SetEnabled.

The items follow the store, not the other way around. On entry into the world, the player's handler 0x19BD8F8 calls 0xE857A0 with the store. For every owned stone whose UnlockItem is missing from the inventory, that function adds the item (AddItemToInventory 0x1C1224); UnlockItem is a WorldPuzzle/SeeingStone field read only by 0xE85340, which only this caller calls. A stone owned in the store therefore restores its fast travel at the next world entry, while an item alone does not light a flame.

The store's owner holds more than the stones:

  • a list of 0x68-byte world-item entries at +0xE8 (0x19CA048, keyed to GameModes.ItemSpawnTransferableToModes), which 0x19C992C walks for Social/Gifts/Save;
  • a list of 0x28-byte entries at +0x00 (0xE85760, called from the player's vf134 0x19BB8BC);
  • a flag at +0x69, set on entering any stone's view and cleared at 0x1D5E938;
  • the data that System::SetItemLocationRevealed reads (0x1A08660).

The stone array itself is written through its lookup 0x19C9014 by exactly two functions, the setter and the loader.

The tower item

In GameDB, 118 cells point at the 22 *_Tower_0* items. For each campaign tower:

  • Regions/TravelPoint::<Region>_0N.UnlockItem unlocks the fast-travel point, and its NavMarkerSetup[].RequiredItem shows the map marker.
  • WorldPuzzle/SeeingStone::SeeingStone_<Region>_0<N+1>.UnlockItem names it: a stone's number is its item's number plus one (15 of 15).
  • Combat/Predicates/Item::TowerPurify_<Region> lists the region's three items, used only by Quest/AutoObjectives::TowerPurify_<Region>.IncludedPlayerAddItemList: the region's progress counts item additions.
  • The Blade of Galadriel side quests Blade_Side02 to _05 require towers (RequiredInventoryItemAll), and three demo loadouts in Inventory/ItemLoadouts (E317, E317_Ellie, WW_Demo) hand them out. Tut_TowerOwned lists all 15 and is used by no record; MinasIthil_QuestTowersActive is the tutorial's marker item.

The GameData switches. All 15 <Region>Tower_0N variables are set by the tutorial, not by climbing: finishing Minas Ithil's tower mission (PostIntro_SR_IdentifiedBarrows) runs the remote script Intro_EnableAllTowers (IMI_EnableAllTowers.Script), which sets every one. A finished save therefore reads all 15 true whether or not the towers were purified. HaedirUnlocked (read by Interface/Predicates/GameState::IsHaedirUnlocked, a menu legend) is not a story gate.

How a Haedir is purified

  • The tower prefab prefabs\gameplay\travel\watchtower_notower.prefab06c (in each region's .scripts): its SO:Init, after a one-second wait, asks self.player:HasInventoryItem(self.UnlockItem). With the item held it does nothing more; without it, it waits for the stone's OnGainOwnership, then plays the tower presentation and runs the region's unlock script. Its FXSO:Init starts the blue flame (Haedir_CB) when SS:IsPlayerOwned() or GameData.DLC_DLC3Active holds, else Sauron's (Haedir_Sauron). Both read their state once, at initialization: a tower that is already loaded keeps its look until its layer loads again.
  • The purify is the first use of the stone's view, 0x1D5E398: if +0x211 is 0 and the current game mode has GameModes.SaveCurrentWorld, it sets +0x211 to 1, calls the store setter 0x19C9BA4, runs the achievement check and raises the presentation and ownership event; the script side then fires.
  • The unlock script in each region's travel.worldlayer06c, Watch_0N_SR.Script1, runs System:GetPlayer():AddInventoryItem(DBInventoryItem.<Region>_Tower_0N, 1) and GameData.<Region>Tower_0N:SetValue(true).
  • The DLC unlocker prefabs\gameplay\quests\dlc3\dlc3haedirunlock.prefab06c, used when the Blade of Galadriel story starts, gives each missing tower the same item and variable, and lights the flame through DLC_DLC3Active rather than through the store.

The achievement. Interface/AchievementSetup.SeeingStoneChecks lists the 15 stones. Its accessor 0x17C3DB0 has one caller, 0x17C6EAC, whose one caller is the purify (0x1D5E48E). Only a purify done at a stone evaluates it; nothing that writes the store does. The records involved are Interface/Achievements::PurifiedHaedir (trophy 27, 20 points), Interface/Collectables::PurifiedHaedir and the profile statistics AchievementsPurifiedHaedir / HaedirPurified in Interface/GameProfileData.

The region challenge. Quest/DynamicChallenge::TowerPurify_<Region> has a stage whose ItemAwards is a skill point (5 of 5 regions) and a goal with CompleteProgress 3 and the automatic objective TowerPurify_<Region> (type 28) counting additions of the region's three tower items. Purifying a region's three Haedirs pays one skill point.

The story gate. Quest/Quests::Intro_Tower (GoldenPathQuest_NoFade, contributes to progress, cannot be quit, after IntroPart30) is the story's first Haedir: it completes when the player identifies the barrows through a purified tower's stone.

Lithlad has no campaign Haedirs. Desert_Tower_00 to _05 unlock Regions/TravelPoint::Desert_00 to _05, which neither the campaign's fast-travel legend (QuestLog_Row6_FastTravel: the fort, the landmarks, the zone exit) nor the online modes' LockedTravelPointsOverride lists; no seeing stone or purify objective names them. (Desert_Tower_01's predicate tests DesertFort_01, a slip in the data.)

The tower's flame and eye

Each tower is a Watch_0N prefab instance. Its objects include the flames FX_SS_CB_World / FX_SS_Sauron_World and their wraith-world versions FX_SS_CB_Wraith / FX_SS_Sauron_Wraith; the eyes FX_Eye_CB / FX_Eye_Sauron (all SpecialFXSeq, vt_0x1DC1530); the transitions FX_SS_Transition / FX_Eye_Transition (SpecialFX); the stone SSP.SS; the script objects FXSO and SO; and the map marker LockedNM. Placed objects are found by name through the update manager's object sets at [base+0x26FF050]+0x3E0+0x18: 128 sets, one per world context (vt_0x1DB5D58), each with its name table at +0x68.

The tower's script (fx_watchtower.script06a) starts the flame once at initialization, by SS:IsPlayerOwned(). Its WraithOnCallback (wraith vision or a presentation) starts the eye and the wraith flame of the matching color, by IsPlayerOwned() at that moment, and its WraithOffCallback stops exactly what it started. So the eye exists only in wraith vision, and after a purify both the flame and the eye keep the old color until the layer initializes again: after advancing the time of day (System.AdvanceTimeOfDay 0x1787D08) or a load.

The SpecialFXSeq class provides Start (0x74F0E4), Shutdown (0x74F1A0), QuickStart (0x1B201F0), QuickShutdown (0x1B201A4) and IsPlaying (0x1B1FEF4). Its sequence player sits at +0x138; the player's vf1 takes 0 to start, 1 to start quickly and 2 to shut down, and IsPlaying is 0x114ED48(+0x138) != -1.

The effects are haedir_cb_loop.fxp (a sprite, a light and two sounds), watchtower_eye_cb_loop.fxp (a light, 3 models and 7 particle systems) and haedir_cb_wraith_loop.fxp (a sprite and 2 particle systems). They use their own materials, such as haedir_glow.mat, cele_eyeball_flame.mat and sauron_eyeball_flame.mat, and textures in color pairs such as cele_eyeflame_v2.tex / sauron_eyeflame_v2.tex. None of them has a MaterialController, so their color lives in the textures and the lights (see Visual effects).
