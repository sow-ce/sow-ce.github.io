The Nemesis system: factions, captains and the army

The faction system and its pools and 38 armies, the FactionMember object and its saved fields, the seat table of every army's rows, the nemesis flag word and its engine setters, faction events, turns and sim events, cheat death and the event history, the ambush queue, rarity and Training Orders, followers, bodyguards and mercenaries, and how story and DLC characters take seats.

Every named orc, olog and story character is a FactionMember owned by the faction system, seated in an army's rows, and changed by faction events that the engine applies to it. The hierarchy, the promotions, the returns from death and the ambushes are data: rows, events and reward lists in the Faction/* categories of the GameDB, driven by a small set of engine functions.

Traits, classes and personalities are in Captain traits, classes and personalities; story characters, betrayal scenes and region ownership in Quests, story and scenes; looks in Captain appearance; what a save keeps in Saves, the store and the network layer.

The faction system

The faction system is FS = [GM+0x6C68] ([[base+0x26FFAA8]+0x6C68]; see Engine core). The scene tick calls 0x1E2BC4 on it every frame (see Threads and the frame); through 0x1E2A58 that also runs the ambush update 0x6DDBA8.

Pools

Members live in pools, one per game mode, not per region: the MainGame pool holds every captain of every region. A pool entry's first qword is its Faction/Pool record; the pool object starts at entry+8 (vt_0x1FD8310) and holds its member vector at +0x18 / +0x20. FM+0x174 is the member's index in that vector (500 of 500 members). The entries follow the Faction/Pool record order (9 of 9: Player, QuestReplay, QuestReplay_Ratbag2, InvasionGame, Test, Demo, DLC3, DLC4, MainGame), so MainGame is entry 8; entry+0xB0 repeats the record and entry+0xB8 holds its MaxMembersToTrack (9 of 9).

The MainGame record also carries importance coefficients (ImpC_XPLevel 1.5, ImpC_RowRank, ImpC_Role, ImpC_ISTotal 1.0). A new member comes from 0x1BD2E20 (reached through 0x1C23C10), which builds the object with 0x19ED468. When the pool is full it recycles its least important member, and never one with a row (+0x268), a preset (+0x190), a current sim event (+0x4F0), a unique builder instance (+0x350) or flag bit 45. A late campaign's MainGame pool sits at its 500 cap; in one such save the recyclable members were 201 dead former followers and 48 unassigned grunts, and no living follower.

Armies

An army is a Faction/Definition record. 38 ship, and every one has a context in every game mode. The context map at FS+0x98 is indexed by the definition's record index: entry+0 is the record and the context is entry+8 (vt_0x1FDDF78). 0x18DE04(map, record) returns it and asserts "Bad record index" when the record belongs to another category.

Runtime rows. The context's rows at ctx+0xF8 / +0x100 are 0x28 bytes, one per definition row:

Offset Meaning
+0x00 the Faction/Rows record
+0x08 / +0x10 the seats: int32 per column, the occupant's pool index, -1 = empty
+0x20 u32 seat count: the record's ColumnMax (208 of 208 live rows across the 38 armies, with the seats vector the same length)
+0x24 u32 column lock bits (tested by 0x24EBB8)

A member's own seat is FM+0x268 (the row record) plus FM+0x51B (the column; 0xFF = rowless). Several members share one row record because a row has several seats; each seat holds exactly one member. The save stores the row as its index in the army's Rows and the column as a byte.

Moving members.

  • Seat 0x61E13C(ctx, fm, row, column, flag): flag 1 = a new member (the creator 0x1C1D378), 0 = a move (the sim outcome 0x40C304). It refuses a move into a warchief row whose side differs from the Overlord's when FM+0x4F8 is set (0x1E4C08 / 0x114E020); it vacates first, re-levels the member for the row (0x1C1BDA8 -> SetLevel 0x35188C), checks that the column is below the seat count, unlocked and empty, then writes the pool index into the seat, FM+0x51B and FM+0x268 (0x61B614).
  • Vacate 0x61B274(ctx, fm): -1 in every seat holding his index, FM+0x51B = 0xFF, FM+0x268 = 0, and the dirty bytes ctx+0x1EE / +0x1F0. 0x61D354(fm, bool) marks a member as moving (FM+0x51F).
  • Swap, the engine's own at 0x40C767 (inside the event-processing code): mark both, vacate B, unlink B's pool node (FM+0x18), seat A into B's seat and B into A's old one (both flag 0), unmark.
  • Transfer between armies is FactionSystem::TransferMember 0xF6AC90(FS, fm, {army, row, column}): it refuses unless both armies share a pool and refuses a move inside one army; it unlinks the member (0x1C25500), rewrites FM+0x178 (and +0x180 when empty) and fires the destination's EventSetup.TransferIntoFaction (every base army ships Transform_HireMercToEmpty). Any two MainGame armies share a pool.

Dead captains keep their seats. In a late campaign the six regional armies held no empty seat and one to three dead captains each. The sim-event seat predicate 0xFDC350 treats an empty seat, or a dead member without flag bit 4, as free ("Passed: Dead") and refuses a dead member with bit 4 ("Failure: Resurrectable"), then checks the preset, spy (bit 28) and follower (bit 27) states, a blocking captor event and a duel. The jobs that take a dead or empty seat (Quest/Jobs/Definition DeadOrEmptyPosition, _WC, _Overlord) require MustHaveCanResurrect False; 26 of the 849 shipped jobs set that field, all to False. A captain who will return therefore keeps his seat.

Filling. FillFaction 0x1C1E13C(ctx) is army initialisation's fill. Per row and per column whose seat holds -1 and whose lock bit is clear, it creates one new member: from the row's InitBuilderRecords by InitBuilderChance (0x1C1E280 -> 0x1C1CFAC), or by the generic build 0x1C1D100 (allocate 0x1C23C10 -> pool 0x1BD2E20, build 0x1BC0084, register in the pool 0x61B5E8, level 0x1C1BDA8 -> SetLevel, seat 0x61E13C with flag 1, the definition's intel, follower and mercenary flags, henchmen 0x7DEEE0). The builder path calls no SetLevel. The latch ctx+0x21C limits the fill to once per initialisation (0x185FE0).

The FactionMember

The FactionMember (vt_0x1FD5EA0) is 0x528 bytes. Both allocation sites ask for 0x528 bytes (0x19ED468: mov ecx,0x528 -> allocator 0x3DE238 -> constructor 0x1BB4C1C; 0xF99FF0 likewise) and the constructor's last store is +0x520. Everything at +0x530 and above belongs to other allocations: the appearance object is a separate object at [FM+0x2D8] (it sat directly after the member for 0 of 206 row captains), and the traits live in vectors (see Captain traits). The constructor allocates that object itself, right after the member (see Captain appearance), so whether it sits at FM+0x530 depends on the heap's history: in another running game it did for 55 of the first 60 seated captains.

Valid member test: [FM+0x178] is a GameDB record (526 of 526 members pass, 0 of 53 other pointers do). FM+0x174 is not a validity flag: the MainGame and DLC pools both hold a member at index 0.

Member keys. The engine refers to a member by the pair {faction record = FM+0x178, u32 pool index = FM+0x174}, not by pointer: faction events, ambush requests and the boss link all store keys.

The document form. Besides the binary save, a member is written and read as a key-named document tree: the writer 0x1BC45A8 (its callers include the console commands FactionMember.Save and FactionMember.Save.Pos and presentation code) and the reader 0x1BC159C (used by the member factory 0xF99FF0 and presentation code). The "document key" names in the table come from this pair; it also names +0x278 CharacterModel, +0x2D8 SMCStorage, +0x500 ResurrectionCount and +0x520 InitializationType. The last-death block is written by 0x1BC4FCC and read by 0x1BC1F50 with FM+0x298 as the base: +0x10 LastDeathCharacterType, +0x18 LastDeathAttack, +0x20 ExitNavMesh, +0x21 LastDeathPlayerKiller.

Names

The name is a string id, FM+0x170. 0x1BC6040(fm, id) stores it, looks up the localized UTF-16 string, converts it with WideCharToMultiByte on the system ANSI code page (CodePage 0, Windows-1252 on a Western system) and copies at most 31 characters into the 32-byte buffer FM+0x498; an id of 0 clears the buffer. A builder-built orc gets his name id from the name table of his class map (_StructuresFactionClassDataMap.NameTable; Faction/NameTable has Orc, Olog, Forthog and Easterling), so +0x498 is a derived copy and the id is what the save keeps.

Levels

FM+0x4B8 is stored 0-based; the game shows it plus one, and caps the shown level at 85. SetLevel 0x35188C ends in 0x353C44, which sets flag bits 30 and 31 when the level reaches two thresholds read from the army; crossing the second with no tribe assigns one (0x1BC6718, reason "XPLevel"). Seating re-levels a member for the row (0x1C1BDA8); a story character without a row starts at NoRowXPLevel - 1.

Finding every captain

  • From the seats. For each army context, each runtime row and each column, a seat value other than -1 is a pool index into the army's pool object (ctx+0x20). This finds exactly the seated members, dead or alive, with their row and column.
  • From the pools. Walk every pool's member vector and keep the valid members ([FM+0x178] is a record). A member with a row (FM+0x268, named <region>_Row_<rank>) is a captain, warchief or Overlord; dead ones have flag bit 2. Story characters carry FM+0x190 and normally no row. Spawned members have FM+0x48.

Pools are by game mode, so the region of a member is his army (FM+0x178) or his row's prefix, never his pool.

The nemesis flag word

FM+0x508 is a u64 of flags. The getter 0x114B55C is a bare bt [fm+0x508], bit; 0x114DFE8 tests bit 2 (dead). The loader's keys name most bits, and the FactionMember document writer 0x1BC45A8 (which writes Identity, Flags, XPLevel ... Inventory) names more: it writes each boolean key from one getter call, IsResurrected bit 0, IsDead 2, KilledByPlayer 3, CanResurrect 4, PresentedToPlayer 5, IdentityPresented 7, HasEverSpawned 9, IgnorePlayerLevelRestriction 10, HasVow 12, SeveredHead 17, IsMockDead 21, CanInterrogateAbout 22, RevengeTarget 24, WasEverPreset 25, Builder_CanNotSave 32, Builder_DisableSimEvents 33, DisableTraitChanges 38, DisableRelationships 39, CanNotDropLoot 40, CanNotChangeXPLevel 41, CanNotApplyContract 42 and CalcXPLevelOnAssignToRow 44, while PlayerFollower, Spy and Prisoner come from functions (0x114E020, 0x1149E50, 0x1BBCAEC) rather than a single bit.

Three bits have no saved key. Bit 37 marks a temporary member: 0x1BBF928 sets it when the builder's byte +0x180 is set, and 0x1BC04E4 sets it on an online arena opponent (__online_arena_opponent__, which also clears the follower bit). A quest's Cleanup_OnEnter (0x7652BC) then calls 0x1B5E0B4, which checks the bit, confirms the member still sits at its pool index, nulls that slot and destroys the member. Bit 35 is set in one place, 0x61CED0, which assigns a member to a sim event: it copies bit 0 of the intel mask FM+0x510 at that moment. In one campaign all 102 members with bit 35 held that mask bit, none of the 75 members without it had bit 35, and 350 members held the mask bit without it. Bit 16 is set by the un-death 0x630644 just before it clears the dead bit, and that function never clears it, but only 1 of the 86 members with a return on record (FM+0x500 above 0) carried it, so it does not mark a returned member. Use FM+0x500 for that.

The document writer 0x1BC45A8 and reader 0x1BC159C name more bits (getter 0x114B55C and setter 0x1156740 with the bit number next to each key): 9 HasEverSpawned (also set by 0x356370), 10 IgnorePlayerLevelRestriction, 32 Builder_CanNotSave, 33 Builder_DisableSimEvents, 36 Prisoner, 38 DisableTraitChanges, 39 DisableRelationships, 40 CanNotDropLoot, 41 CanNotChangeXPLevel, 42 CanNotApplyContract and 44 CalcXPLevelOnAssignToRow. FM+0x50C is no separate field: it is the word's upper half (the constructor stores +0x508 as one qword; 40 of 40 seated captains agree).

The engine's setters do more than the bit, so a direct write to the word skips their side effects:

  • SetDead 0x626E38(fm, dead) sets bit 2 and, when FM+0x398 names a campaign variable, sets that variable (0x59A1D8): a story character's death unlocks the next scene this way. Its un-death branch (0x627197) clears bits 2, 12 and 17 but not 21.
  • The follower setter 0x7D52B0(fm, bool) changes the member; the character's CCharacter::SetIsPlayerFollower 0x102F0C(char, bool) also sets the character's follower byte (+0x23B8) and its combat stats before calling it. Region ownership is the Overlord's bit 27 (see Quests, story and scenes).
  • The intel setter 0x353EC4(fm, level, fireEvent, force) refuses a level that is not higher unless forced, clamps to the setup's DataStages, writes FM+0x51A, rebuilds the mask FM+0x510 (0x62136C), notifies the player (0x7BE290, which reaches the global script agent [GM+0x6CF0], so it belongs on the main thread), updates trait visibility on a body, fires the army's DataLevelChangeEvent when asked, and re-resolves his traits (UpdateTraits reason 6).
  • Bit 32's clear 0x18E218 is a bare btr with no event.

The pool serializer 0x59460C writes every member unless 0x595890(fm) (= not bit 32) refuses him; 0x595CA0 is the only other reader of bit 32. Members held by the online garrison carry it.

Faction events

Everything that happens to a member is a Faction/Events/Events record (2,039 ship, each with the same 77 fields) applied by the engine:

  1. System::CreateAndProcessFactionEvent 0x1B5B610(FS, event, subject, other) builds a 0x60-byte descriptor holding the subject's key, the other's key (index -1 when there is none), the event and the other's row index (0x1E4D90) and column, then calls 0x40C0AC(FS, descriptor). The script binding 0x1B7F210 resolves its handles in the current region's pool first.
  2. 0x40C0AC processes the event synchronously; an event raised while another is in progress (FS+0x208) is queued at FS+0x1F0 and drained afterwards (post-processing 0x1E2DA0).
  3. 0x40C13C -> 0x62E670, the dispatcher, picks and records the event's replay (the Army-screen flashback: 0x7421A0 -> 0x741284, [SIMEVENT|REPLAY]), then the apply 0x630644 changes the member.

Turns and sim events

The missions on the Army screen (power struggles, hunts, executions, fort assaults) are sim events, and they advance in turns, not in real time.

  • AdvanceTurn 0x1B5EE88(FS, unused, u8 flushPending) increments the turn FS+0x218, on a flush runs 0x1C1C4E4 on every army (draining ctx+0x140), then runs 0xFDEC20(ctx, save slot), turn processing and generation (FactionSimEventGenerate 0x597A9C), for every army that passes 0x1B5B058. That test refuses an army with AdvanceTODCurrentFaction unless it is the current region's army (FS+0x1B8), and otherwise requires the army's AdvanceTODStoryActive to match a state read through [GM+0x6D30] (0x114E008); the campaign armies ship with the first False and the second True. AdvanceTurn's callers are the quest post-active state 0x19F8C34, the world reset 0x1789F0C (the player's death), the single-hierarchy view 0x434CF0 (no flush) and 0x1B5ED08. The world clock stops while the Army screen is open.
  • Generation. For each member, his row's SimEventGenerationSetup lists entries that are tried in order (0x5924EC; each entry may require a follower, a non-follower or either). An entry's Faction/Events/SimEventGenerationType records (68 ship) are picked by weight (a reservoir pick 0x1B3B90 in 0x5926EC; a failure drops the type and retries) and 0x5935DC creates the event. A type's weight is Weights[min(active events of that type in the army, count - 1)], or its WeightOverride's entries when set (difficulty-scaled through SimEventGeneration_* constants); weight 0 is the cap. PowerStruggle_Act2 lists 13 sim events (Chase, Duel, Execution2, GraugHeist, the raids, the trials ...) with Weights 10, 7, 4, 0 and the global-variable predicate Act2. Candidate lists are capped at 64 (0x597E44); members with a current event (FM+0x4F0), dead members without bit 4, and members with bit 21 are skipped; one event per member.
  • The event object (vt_0x1DBC8A8): +0x40 the army, +0x48 the Faction/Events/SimEvent (431 ship), +0x50 / +0x58 its quest (a Quest/Quests record such as VO_Chase_02), +0x60 the generation type, +0x94 a serial number and +0x98 the turn it expires (created turn plus the type's lifespan). An army's events are at ctx+0x90 / +0x98.

The cooldown table

Each member can hold a cooldown table [FM+0x258]: a 0x18-byte object {entries, u32 count, category} of {record, u32 expiry turn} entries, the category being Faction/Events/SimEventCooldown ([base+0x2701A18]; two records, PowerStruggle_CD and HuntingMission_CD). The gate 0x1BC0C2C(fm, SimEvent.RequiredCooldown) passes when there is no table or the entry has expired, and refuses when the table exists but has no entry (the lookup 0x1BBC410 returns null). The setter 0x1BB7578 creates and sizes a table (0x1BBF840) only when none exists. The FactionMember loader creates the table when the save flags one (0x1BC0F2C) but never sizes it, and reads each saved entry into a temporary (0x1BC0EC4). After a load, every member that had a cooldown is refused power struggles and hunts permanently: in a late-campaign pool of 500, no table was sized and 60 living seated captains carried an empty one. An emulated generation pass on Minas Ithil's army, with every captain free and no events, refused 204 of 221 attempts; every power-struggle and hunt candidate (340 of 340) failed at this gate, the rest at their campaign gates.

Cheat death and the return

Death and return run through the kill path and events:

  1. Mark. The kill path 0x6DB95C (0x1BB9F84 for event kills) stores the death's Faction/Resurrection record at FM+0x2C0 and, when the row has ResurrectionEnabled, rolls the odds (0x6DBD4C: presets refused, row, predicate, odds table). On success the evaluator 0x1BC5D44 picks a fitting scar (0x1B5AA00; none = "No Available Scars", nothing changes), increments FS+0x21C and FM+0x500, sets bit 4, stores the scar at FM+0x348 (unless it equals FM+0x370) and fires the record's DeathEvent through 0x40C0AC. Without ResurrectionEnabled the kill path resets the mock member (0x6DBCFC).
  2. Queue. The death event (WillResurrect_<death>, 23 of them, KeepPriority 30) carries reward lists that queue the ambush: CheatDeath_Ambush for an enemy, CheatDeath_Betrayal or CheatDeath_FollowerStillLoyal for a follower. CheatDeath_Ambush retries forever (RetryMax -1, RetryDelay 0.25 minutes), never for an Overlord, and fires only in its own region; one such request reached 1,121 retries. The return is not a job: no shipped job requires MustHaveCanResurrect True.
  3. Complete. Only the apply 0x630644 un-deaths: when the member has bit 4 and the event has Resurrect, or the event has PlayerResurrect, it clears bits 4 and 6, sets 0 and 16, clears 2 and 17, then runs 0xE7DF70(fm). Resurrected_Presented (the ambush's success event) adds SetMockMemberDead False, ClearCopyFactionMemberData, PickNewSimEvent, one level plus a player-based level change, and EjectIfWarchief / EjectIfOverlord.

Other readers of bit 4: the seat predicate 0xFDC350, the cancel pass 0xF9BA70 (from 0xF665F0 and 0xFA0D30: a dead member with bit 4 that fails 0xFF3D30 loses bits 4, 0 and 16), the sever abort 0x6DE30C ("Aborting Resurrection, Faction member was severed after death", only while the corpse is in the world), SetDead, the writer 0x1BC5044 and the FactionMember document writer 0x1BC45A8 (whose keys include CanResurrect).

The data. Faction/Resurrection has 34 death types. Each names an OddsTable (Faction/Resurrection/Tables: Base, Bleedout, BodyCleaved_Uruk, Death_from_Fall, HeadRemoved_Olog, over 14 Faction/Resurrection/Chance records), a ScarType, a SecondScarChance and SecondScarType, a DeathEvent and loot tags. The odds roll (0x6DC65C, called from 0x6DBD4C) walks the table's ChanceRecords and keeps a Faction/Resurrection/Chance record only when the world total FS+0x21C lies within its MinTotalResurrected .. MaxTotalResurrected and the member's own count FM+0x500 within MinThisGuyResurrected .. MaxThisGuyResurrected (0x6DC784; a negative bound is no bound). The shipped records use the world total as a campaign stage and the member's count as a repeat counter:

Chance record World total Member's count
Resurrection_1st, Resurrection_1st_FallOnly 0 any
Resurrection_2ndFew, Resurrection_2ndFew_FallOnly 1 - 4 any
Resurrection_3rdFew, Resurrection_3rdFew_Bleedout 5 or more any
Resurrection_1stFew_OlogHeadRemoved / _2ndFew_OlogHeadRemoved up to 2 / 3 or more any
ResurrectSameGuy_Again / _Again2 / _Again3 any 1 - 2 / 2 - 3 / 4 or more
ResurrectSameGuy_Again_OlogHeadRemoved / _Again2_OlogHeadRemoved any 1 - 2 / 2 or more

From the fifth return of a campaign on, the world total is above every upper bound (it read 375 in one late campaign), so only the records without one stay eligible: the 3rdFew pair, _2ndFew_OlogHeadRemoved, Resurrection_BodyCleave and the same-member records. Resurrection_Torment: table Base, scar Curse, a second scar (Sack_Head or Metal_Head) at 0.5, event WillResurrect_Torment. Resurrection_HeadRemoved has no odds table and no death event: a decapitated orc never returns (the olog has its own table).

The mock member. FM+0x2E0 is a 0x20-byte copy {appearance, inventory, role, flag} that the Army screen draws instead of the real member while it exists (0x75D2FC / 0x75D314 read it). Rewards with CopyAndHideFactionMemberData build it (22 of 1,239 rewards: role transforms, pinnacle scars), as does an event's SetMockMemberDead True; ClearCopyFactionMemberData, SetMockMemberDead False and the kill path without resurrection drop it. Bit 21 says the mock member is dead. Every death in a resurrection row keeps the copy whether or not the member will return; among living members only one returned but not yet presented carries it, together with bit 21 (SimResurrected sets Resurrect without touching the mock member). In one late save the 11 seated dead members all had bit 21 and 9 of them a copy; of 197 seated living members, one (returned, not yet presented) had a copy and bit 21.

Raising the dead. Resurrected_PlayerAbility_RaiseDead, the only event with PlayerResurrect, un-deaths any member, scars him Resurrected_PlayerAbility_RaiseDead, adds a level, aborts his ambushes and rolls PlayerSkill_Resurrected_Captain: one of four rewards giving tribe TribeDef_Undead, the undead items, personality Damaged901_Undead_pss_Moaner, deranged markers, a permanent fire vulnerability, poison immunity and Unbreakable, after removing every fire and poison trait. It does not touch the follower bit.

The event history

FM+0x300 / +0x308 is the member's history: 16-byte entries {Faction/Events/Events record, u16 the turn}, in order (488 of 488). 0x631648 trims it whenever it exceeds 32 entries through 0x1BC3EB8, which erases the entry with the lowest KeepPriority, the oldest on a tie; the save writes 32 slots (0x594788). KeepPriority is read only by that eviction. Shipped priorities: the WillResurrect_* death events and Resurrected_Presented 30, ActiveKilledByPlayer 10, ActiveKillPlayer_grunt 100, and 50 for frequent events such as ActiveInitialPlayerThreat, ActiveKillNamedAI, FleeRunStart and every fear or hate trigger, so a full history drops its deaths before them: 196 of 515 histories were at the cap, and 18 of 86 members who had cheated death had lost the record of it, all 18 at the cap. A spawned character's audio object receives a copy of the list (see Audio and video).

Ambushes

The ambush manager is [FS+0x30]. TriggerAmbush 0x6DDCB8(manager, definition, forced member) only queues a 0x40-byte request in the vector at +0x338:

Request offset Meaning
+0x18 the region's army it was queued in
+0x20 the Faction/Ambush/Definition
+0x28 the forced ambusher's key ({0, -1} = none)
+0x38 u32 due time in milliseconds on the world clock
+0x3C retries

A second request for a queued definition is refused unless its forced member is dead (0x114DFE8); one for the active definition is refused. The update 0x6DDBA8 runs only while no ambush is active ([manager] holds the active one) and evaluates each due request with 0x6DD92C: a forced member already in the world is discarded; the gates are the global enable [base+0x267D598], manager+0x368 (the running quest allows ambushes), +0x369 (the player is not in a FactionAmbushDisableBrush) and the post-quest timer +0x36C; a request whose army is not the current region's is retried (see Quests, story and scenes). Then 0x1C5E358: the chance (0x1C5F0E8: AmbushChance, the global-variable predicate, the player's excluded attacks, tags and buffs, minimum followers, the Overlord-follower predicate), the ambusher 0x1C5D458 (the forced one unless he is spawned or fails the job, else a job pick 0x1B5AC8C; 16 definitions with NeverPickAmbusherWithJob or no job can only fire with a provided ambusher), validation 0x1C5F070 (not spawned, rank not in ExcludedRowRanks) and the result 0x1C5D624. A failed pass re-queues the request after RetryDelay minutes. A rowless member's rank is null, so an empty ExcludedRowRanks refuses every rowless ambusher. The active ambush object (vt_0x1FFACB0) keeps +0x38 the ambusher, +0x90 the definition, +0xA5 completed and +0xA6 done; its slot 2 0x1C7E1FC only sets +0xA6, after which the manager's tick drops the request and deletes the object.

Rarity and Training Orders

FM+0x390 is a Faction/Traits/MarkerRarity record: OrcRarity_Common, OrcRarity_Epic, OrcRarity_Legendary and a test record OrcRariry_Unique (display name test_string). Each gives the hierarchy's selection effect (Interface/ElementFXList/Rarity_*), a priority (0, 1, 2) and a tint (Epic 0xFFED5132, Legendary 0xFF4509C4). Rarity is also a trait: Picker_OrcRarity_* -> the OrcRarity_* trait -> the item Buff_OrcRarity_*. A Legendary carries two epic traits (ET_*) and an ET_CanRemove_* marker, an Epic one.

A Training Order is an event. Contract_LegendaryTraining adds one level and rolls the reward lists Contract_LegendaryTraining_CommonOrc, _EpicOrc, Default_EpicTraits_PickRemovable and _MarkerSwap; the events Upgrade_OrcRarity_ToEpic and _ToLegendary are the other upgrades. The marker swap LT_MarkerSwap is eligible only for a member who already has an epic trait (IncludedTraitPicker PickerListRaw_AllEpicTraits), so applying it directly through ApplyReward makes a Legendary with no epic traits; rolling the lists through 0x350500 gives the traits first. Entering an Overlord row applies the same kind of upgrade: its RewardListOnEnterRow rolls OverlordSetup_LegendaryUpgrade_CommonOrc (twice), _EpicOrc, Default_EpicTraits_PickRemovable, Contract_LegendaryTraining_MarkerSwap and Overlord_ElementalVent_Resistances.

Tribes, roles and builders

  • Tribes are Faction/Tribes/Definitions (19): Dark, Feral, Machine, Marauder, Militant, Outlaw, Slaughter, Sorcerous, Terror, Warmonger, Undead, Easterling, Tribeless, the special tribes Bruz, Gondor, Sauron, ShadowWar and NazgulTwins, and DLC_TribeDef_Sorcerous_Poison. A member gets one when he is built (0x1BC6718, reason "Builder") or crosses the level threshold without one.
  • Roles (FM+0x318, 914 Faction/Roles/Definitions) carry the title; the role setter 0x1BC63F8 writes +0x318 and +0x320.
  • Builders. A Faction/FactionMemberBuilder (608 ship) is a recipe: CombatTrees, Roles, Tribe / IncludedTribes, ArmorRowRank, MinLevel (+ MinLevelDelta, PlayerBasedXPLevel), CalcXPLevelOnAssignToRow, DataLevel, trait markers, desired and excluded trait pickers, RandomTraitPickerLists, personalities, Rewards / RewardLists, SimEventPickers, PlayerFollower, CanSave, RemoveAfterQuest, EnableTraitChanges, EnableRelationships, UniqueBuilderInstance. Captain (MinLevel 5) and EmergencyOverlord (MinLevel 30 +/- 5, enters through Builder_EnterHierarchy) are the base ones; families such as FD_InvCap_*, DLC5_Y<n>_<region>_C<n>_*, SW_Y* and Gauntlet_* serve other game modes. A build request is prepared by 0x1C63920, which stores the builder at request +0x168, its UniqueBuilderInstance at +0x140 and its flags; the builder build 0x1BBF928 then copies the builder to FM+0x3A0 and the instance to FM+0x350, sets +0x520 = 2, and writes the seed, a name from the class map, a personality (0x1BDA7AC), forces the intel level to 0 (0x353EC4 with force), assigns a tribe, the follower and level-cap flags, then levels him and resolves his traits.

Followers, bodyguards and mercenaries

  • Followers. Bit 27; set and cleared by events (SetPlayerFollower, ToggleIsPlayerFollower) and by the follower setter 0x7D52B0, which CreateEmergencyPlayerFollower uses. A betrayal clears it.
  • Bodyguards. Only the Captain rank has CanBePlayerBodyguard. Bit 29 marks the player's bodyguard (the event BecomeBodyguard is the one with ChangePlayerBodyguard), and bits 48-50 give the summon slot read by 0x1010790 together with the army's PlayerBodyguardSummonDataSlot0-2. Overlords and warchiefs have their own bodyguards: their rows' henchmen, made by 0x7DEEE0 when the army fills or a preset with henchmen is built.
  • Spies and prisoners are event flags (SetIsSpy, bit 28; SetIsPrisoner).
  • Mercenaries. A hire is a transfer, not a new member. The mercenary screen's action 0xDBC910 takes the member from Faction_desert_mercs_DLC4, prices him (0xFA3AF0: the row's HireCost through the army's HireCostVariable / HireCostOption, scalar 1.0 without discount skills), pays (0x189353C) in the currency item of the player's skill setup (PC_Mirian for Talion), then calls TransferMember 0xF6AC90. A zero price passes the spend check 0x1BCEC24. The mercenary army fills its members as followers and mercenaries (bits 27 and 51). It binds the DLC4 pool and belongs to the DLC4 save slot, so the base campaign never fills it.

Story and DLC characters in the hierarchy

A story character is a Faction/PresetMembers record (203; 67 belong to the DLCs). The saves of the base campaign hold the DLC cast too: the mercenary army, the ten Blade of Galadriel twin armies and their presets (the Nazgul sisters DLC3_Nazgul_Twin_1, eight DLC3ToMain_* specialists, DLC_FakeDarkTalion, DLC4_Desert_Overlord). The base game's Nazgul are presets as well (six GP_Nazgul_*). The build sequence is in Quests, story and scenes; the seating law is the creator's:

The creator seats any preset by its FactionRow. 0x1C1D378(ctx, preset) allocates (0x1C23C10), builds (0x1BC06C0: +0x190 = the preset, +0x3A0 = 0, +0x520 = 3, flag 25) and registers the member (0x61B5E8). With no row he is rowless at NoRowXPLevel - 1 (0x1C21448). With a row he is levelled for it (0x1C1BDA8), the column is the preset's FactionColumn (0x1C206AC); if that seat is taken (0x4F9014) or the column is negative, the row (0x61AC00) gives a free column (0x1C1BEB4), and the seat call 0x61E13C(flag 1) runs only when a free column exists: in a full row the character is built but unseated. Then each preset event is fired through 0x40C0AC. Its callers are army initialisation 0xFDAC54 (the definition's PresetMembers), the henchmen builder 0x1BB7898 (PresetHenchmen) and the quest path 0x62666C. Seated this way, a preset takes the row's level and his own tribe (a Blade of Galadriel preset in a Seregost warchief row: level 84, TribeDef_NazgulTwins).

DLC_FakeDarkTalion (AI_Talion, IsDarkTalion, LockedXPLevel 60) has the build event Debug_RecruitFollower, which makes him a follower: seated as an Overlord he hands the region to the player. An occupied Overlord seat is emptied synchronously by an event with EjectIfOverlord through 0x1B5B610; the engine re-places the ejected Overlord in a captain row shortly afterwards.

Unknown captains and intel

FM+0x51A is the intel level (0-3 in play), and FM+0x510 the intel mask that 0x62136C rebuilds from it with the army's InterrogationSetup (Default, three DataStages): level 0 gives 0x2, level 1 0x9B, levels 2 and 3 0xFB (3, 30 and 531 of 564 members). Mask bit 0 means the name is known; UnlockName 0x621208 sets it. The Army screen draws a generic model (Character/Models/HierarchyUnknown) for a member whose name is not known and who has never killed the player (see User interface). A level-0 start is ordinary: the builder build, 0x1BC00C8, 0x1BC02AC, the preset build (twice) and System::HierarchyResetPreSetMemberData 0x1C2373C force it, and the war simulation (0xFCD340, 0xFD0660) forces it whenever its level is lower. The hierarchy treats bit 21 (mock dead) as eliminated: the henchmen link effects 0x18316FC / 0x183191C skip such a member.

Open questions

  • What FM+0x4F8 means. The seat function tests it; it held 1 on 2 of 555 members in one campaign, both in the InvasionGame pool, and 0 on 527 of 527 in another.
  • Why flag bit 16 is missing from most returned members. A rebuild does not drop it: the bit stayed on the same member while 516 of 528 member objects were rebuilt at new addresses.
  • What reads flag bit 35. It has no resolved getter call, and 60 of the getter's 125 calls pass the bit indirectly.
  • Whether the role counter at FS+0xB0 lowers the role's weight by WeightReductionPerUse per use, as the scar table does by its use count; the code that reads it is not traced.
  • Whether the pool's ImpC_* coefficients are the importance score used when a full pool recycles a member.
  • Whether the world's resurrection total FS+0x21C is saved with the game, and under which key (the faction manager's document keys include Resurrected).
  • When exactly the cancel pass 0xF9BA70 runs relative to a save and a load. Its caller 0xFA0D30 is reached from two places: the faction manager's part of the game document (0x1B5D254, keys TimeStamp, Resurrected, FactionPool, FactionRecord, FactionRole, AmbushMgr, GauntletMgr, MonumentMgr), which the document routine 0x1C5B4C4 runs together with the quest manager, the game variables and the inventory, and the online-fortress setup's finalisation (0x1B40CAC -> 0x1B5D368, stage SETUP_FINALIZE_VALIDATE_DATA_INTEGRITY). The direction of the document routine (write, read or both) is not traced; the second caller 0xF665F0 has no direct caller.
  • Whether a queued ambush request's retry count survives a save and reload.
  • Which loading screens rebuild the army contexts and members: values changed in the mercenary army's context and records survived two region changes.
