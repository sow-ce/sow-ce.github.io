Comparison with Shadow of Mordor

How Shadow of Mordor (2014) differs from Shadow of War at the file and engine level: the executable, the archive format and its compression, container and asset format versions, the GameDB, the orc skeletons, materials and textures, the Army screen's arena, and the Mordor-to-War nemesis importer that ships inside Shadow of War.

Shadow of Mordor (2014) is the predecessor engine generation. Its internal project name is Firebird; Shadow of War's is Kraken. The two games share their archive family, container idea, GameDB format and much of their orc rig, but nearly every asset format moved on by several versions, so no Mordor asset loads in War unchanged.

The executables

Shadow of Mordor Shadow of War
x64 executable ShadowOfMordor.exe, 27,930,624 bytes ShadowOfWar.exe, 117,732,784 bytes
Load address 0x140000000 preferred, relocatable (DYNAMIC_BASE, relocation table present) always 0x140000000 (relocations stripped)
Code a conventional .text section of about 20.6 MB .arch (about 30 MB) plus 70 MB of code in .bss a (see The executable)

Because Mordor's executable is relocatable, its addresses move with ASLR every run; War's static addresses never move.

Archives

Both games use LTAR archives. Mordor's are version 3 (.arch05), War's version 4 (.arch06, see Archives and file formats). The Mordor install has 66 archives, 45.4 GB.

header (0x18 bytes)  "LTAR" | u32 version (3) | u32 names size | u32 folder count | u32 file count | u32 1
names                null-separated strings from 0x18; the last string may run past the declared size,
                     and a small block precedes the entry table
entry (0x20 bytes)   u64 data offset | u64 compressed size | u64 uncompressed size | u32 flag (9) | u32 name offset

The entry table is found by its signature (the flag 9 at +0x18 of consecutive entries). Parsing all 66 archives gives 28,884 entries, equal to the 28,884 declared in the headers; every entry's data lies inside its file and the data offsets ascend in all 28,828 consecutive pairs. A name offset may point into the middle of a string, and an entry's name does not reliably describe its payload, so asset paths are taken from the containers' own path tables, as in War.

Compression. Mordor stores each entry as a stream of chunks {u32 compressed length, u32 uncompressed length (at most 65,536), data} compressed with zlib; chunks whose two lengths are equal are stored uncompressed (of 192 sampled chunk headers, 166 began with the zlib marker and 26 were stored). Chunks are padded, so the next chunk header can sit a few bytes after the previous chunk's data. War compresses with Oodle.

Containers and asset formats

Mordor's containers are BNDL version 4 (War: version 6); 8,034 of them hold 67,982 asset paths (66,731 distinct). Mordor's world prefabs are PRFB version 5 and its Scaleform movies GFX version 8 (ActionScript 2). Asset formats are older versions that War's loaders reject:

Format Shadow of Mordor Shadow of War
mesh (MMSH) 9 16-17
skeleton (SKEL) 8 (War's loader rejects it)
texture (TEXR) 3 8
material instance (LTMI) 4 7-8

Mordor's version-9 mesh vertex is 60 bytes: half-precision position (4 halves), 16-bit texture coordinates, 32-bit normal, tangent and bitangent, a colour, blend weights in BGRA order and bone indices; strides can differ between a mesh's elements. Every Mordor mesh has one UV set, while War's orc gear shaders read a second one. Mordor's texture coordinates can run to 2.0. Textures use the same DDS-based payload, and War's texture header adds a 23-byte block.

Materials are authored differently: Mordor's orcs use about 21 generic shaders, War's use the dedicated nemesis gear, head and body shader trees (see Rendering). For the one weapon compared directly (the Master Pike), Mordor's albedo was about half as bright as War's equivalent (mean 56 / 45 / 39 against 108 / 93 / 82).

The GameDB

Mordor's GameDB is a GADB version 17 file (the same container version as War's; see GameDB) of 23,264,671 bytes inside hotchunk.arch05's globaldatabase bundle: the entry after the named manifest entry, a BNDL v4 wrapping the database. It holds 115 character models named Cha_*. The Black Númenórean characters are three unique models: Cha_Belroth (the Hammer), Cha_Imrakhad (the Tower) and Cha_Zainzadan (the Black Hand, whose mesh one of Talion's alternative skins in Mordor is built from). Mordor ships no generic Black Númenórean soldier armour: a census of every container path (66,731 distinct), every self-named texture (12,714, none unreferenced) and every XML manifest (8,071) finds none.

The warband downloads (layer52 to layer56 archives) are 216-byte unlock stubs: the Guardian, Blood Hunter, Crusher and Flesh Burner orcs are GameDB presets assembling base-game pieces with paint, not new assets.

The 72 shared bones sit at the same bind positions (median difference 0.07 cm, at most 0.94 cm for the core bones), but 36 of them differ in bind rotation (the forearm twist by 81 degrees). The face rigs share no joint names. Of Mordor's orc pieces, those that use only bones orc_base also has: armour 35 of 40, flair 116 of 131, bodies 15 of 15, heads 44 of 111.

The Army screen

Mordor's hierarchy screen is a world of its own, worlds\cinematics\wraith_visions\hierarchymenu_son.bndl (son.arch05 entry 15, 232,667,989 bytes, 446 assets; near-identical copies in intro.arch05 and outro.arch05): a scene, an object database, a world mesh and 10 prefabs (the hierarchy art, the Warchief pedestals, glyphs, orc light posts, a ruin wall, backdrop rocks), with the crowd drawn as card meshes (fx\ui\hierarchy\models\orcarmy_outlinecards.mesh). The seats are logical rows and columns in _StructuresFaction.HierarchyFactionPos (5 Warchiefs and captain rows of 8, 7 and 5: 25 seats), placed in the world as objects Hier.Faction_<row>_<column> with a character spot .Chr, a camera .Cam and about 20 points of interest each. The screen's movie is interface\flash\widgets\hierarchy.gfx, which holds no logic.

The Mordor importer in Shadow of War

Shadow of War ships an importer, the Nemesis Forge, that rebuilds a player's Shadow of Mordor follower and nemesis as War captains. It is compiled from ...\KRAKEN_CODE_STREAM\source\Game\Shared\NemesisForgeUtils.cpp (log tag [NEMESISFORGE]:; the logger is compiled out of the shipping build). The captains come from the publisher's online service, never from Mordor's local save, and they enter the game through two story missions.

Shadow of Mordor's side

The two games never exchange data directly: neither executable names the other game, and the profile id Shadow of War can attach to its request (FirebirdProfileID, [0x2A364F0]) is a static string initialised empty that no code writes, so the request identifies the player by platform user id only. Shadow of Mordor reaches the publisher's earlier online platform, Agora (Social/Agora, AgoraUserDataMgr, ProfileAttributeData); Shadow of War reaches its successor; the publisher's servers joined the two through the player's account.

In Shadow of Mordor the Nemesis Forge is a game mode, GameModes/NemesisForge, started from the main menu (MM_NemesisForge), with its own restart, continue and quit prompts, a pause page and an add-on description. It has its own army, Orc_Faction_NemesisForge: 32 tracked members, the five orc classes, and rows of 5 warchiefs and 8, 7 and 5 captains placed in the Sea of Nurnen. CanPopulateNemesisForge is true on exactly the two campaign armies (Orc_Faction_BG, Orc_Faction_SoN) and false on the downloadable and challenge armies, while NemesisForgeFromThisFaction is true only on the forge army, and the Sea of Nurnen's side missions (trials, duels, beast hunts, riots, executions, assassinations, recruitment, feasts, betrayals, rescues) list the forge army among their required factions. A debug command, DebugSpawnAI.NemesisForge.FetchNemesis, is described as querying the forge nemesis from Agora. Shadow of Mordor does not use Shadow of War's key names: the 13-key reply was built on the server.

The switch and the request

The title configuration's boolean nemesis_forge is stored at [GM+0x6D48]+0xD1 by the configuration parser 0x18368B0 (next to challenges_enabled at +0xD0 and conquests at +0xD2). The check 0x1B3ABFC has one caller, the world-entry routine 0x1E3E78, and works on the online manager [GM+0x6D30]. It does nothing while either stored captain is valid (+0x38, +0x80), or while Invasion/Settings.HaltNemesisForgeCheckPredicate (the settings record at +0x468) holds: that is IsPastNemesisForgeUsage, a test of StoryState against 300, the end of the campaign. Otherwise it tail-jumps to the request 0x1B3AA94, which returns unless the switch is set and no request is in flight (+0x28). The request carries the player's FirebirdProfileID (when one is known), the platform user id, the platform and the web environment, and registers 0x1B32CD0 as its completion handler. The game therefore asks on every world entry until it holds a captain or the campaign is over.

The reply and the two stored captains

The completion handler 0x1B3D500 expects a list whose second element is a map holding two lists, Follower and Nemesis; the first object of each carries an attributes map. The parser 0x1B3A148 reads 13 keys from each map into a 0x48-byte payload (vtable 0x1FCB338) and sets its valid byte last. The online manager keeps both:

Offset Meaning

+0x30 the follower payload (valid byte at +0x38)
+0x78 the nemesis payload (valid byte at +0x80)
+0x468 the Invasion/Settings record

+0x0C CombatTreeRecord name hash the class template and War's Combat/Tree

Every record value is the 32-bit name hash of a Shadow of Mordor record. No look, colour or memory travels in the payload.

The two missions

Quest jobs ask for the stored captains. The job filler 0x1BD40C4 tests three flags of Quest/Jobs/Definition before filling a job the ordinary way: UseNemesisForgeAlly, UseNemesisForgeNemesis and UseOnlineArenaOpponent. Of the 849 job records, one carries each forge flag:

Job Flag Takes Mission Ordinary builder
Rat3_Ally1 UseNemesisForgeAlly the follower Ratbag3, "Damaged": an allied captain while the player retakes the fortress from Brûz Rat3_BackupAlly1
CapChief_HighPIS UseNemesisForgeNemesis the nemesis Gondor2, "The Arena": a champion in Minas Ithil's arena CapChief_HighPIS

With a valid payload the filler creates the captain from it (0xF636F0), keys the job to him and sends a telemetry event (0x1B2CC68, event type 42, with is_follower). Without one, the job's builder record makes an ordinary captain, so both missions play the same either way.

The missions' world scripts stage the result. In "Damaged" (ratbag3_RA03_SO_Script.Follower1Spawn, with the prefab followercage.prefab06c) the ally waits in a cage; the script fires the faction event AddNemesisAlly (one level more, and the sim-event tag NemesisAlly) and adds the sim event picker NemesisAlly_EnterHierarchy, whose duels bring him into the hierarchy. In "The Arena" (gondor2_Go02_SO_Script.Captain4Spawn) the nemesis is the fourth captain, and the script uses the items GP_Gon2NemForgeCheck and NemesisStory_NemesisForge_AuxBanner_IT, whose UI data NemesisStoryUI_NemesisForge gives his nemesis story a banner (UI_Nemesis_Forge, "Nemesis").

The conversion

0xF636F0 takes the army of the current region and calls 0xFD51A0, which takes a member from the army's pool, sets his faction and calls 0x1BC05D0. That runs the conversion 0x1C6369C and then the ordinary FactionMember builder 0x1BBF928 (see The Nemesis system). The conversion fills the builder's specification:

  • Class: the tree hash selects one of the five NemesisForge/FirebirdSimpleModel templates and War's Combat/Tree record of the same name.
  • Personality: FirebirdFactionPersonality.KrakenPersonality; a hash with no row maps to nothing.
  • Title: FirebirdFactionRole.KrakenRole. A hash with no row is searched again in War's own Faction/Roles/Definitions (914 records) by the same hash (0x1C770BC), so a Mordor title that War also has comes through as War's record.
  • Level: max(MinXPLevel, MenaceLevel + 1) - 1 (0x114FFD0 is an unsigned maximum; every template's MinXPLevel is 14).
  • Name: kept only when War's string database holds the hash (its has-test, slot 8); otherwise the builder gives him a War name. 0xFD51A0 removes the new member again when the hash is one of five keys reserved for Ratbag.
  • Scar type and traits: FirebirdModelScarType.KrakenScarType; each trait through FirebirdFactionTrait.KrakenTraitPicker, a trait with no row skipped; then the template's TraitMarkers (Marker_OrcRarity_Legendary).

FactionMember +0x520 records how a member was made and is saved with him: 0x1BC05D0 writes 4 after the builder's own 2 (the preset path writes 3; two other creation paths write 0 and 1). The template's CreationSimEventPicker, NemesisAlly_Levels, introduces him through a sim event that promotes him into an empty seat (PromoteEmpty_* events, nav markers always shown); the payload's row is never used.

The look

No look travels in the payload. The builder's model step 0x1C04F20 rebuilds one from RandomSeed, replaying Shadow of Mordor's generator over the Firebird tables:

  • Scale: the low 32 bits of a xorshift of the seed (shifts 13, 7, 17), times 2^-32.
  • Random stream: a 64-bit linear congruential generator, state * 0x0BB38435 + 0x3619636B, whose value is (state >> 32) / 4294967295, started at the seed (0x1C76F8C). The same constants appear throughout both games' executables.
  • Weighted picks (0x1C767E0): one draw, then the first entry whose running weight reaches it; a total weight below 0.0001 picks nothing.
  • Order: body (the template's Models entries whose RequiredScaleRange holds the scale, weighted by the body piece's SelectionWeight), armour, head, helmet, hair (only with CanApplyHair), legs, one draw per accessory, then the colours. The armour, helmet, leg and accessory draws only advance the generator: those parts are not imported.
  • Colours: for each of the nine channels a weighted Firebird colour entry gives a War colour setup and one of 15 colour ids (Red, White, Fleshtone ... Purple). War's entry with that FirebirdColorId (94 of War's 542 colour entries carry one) is rolled with its HueDeltaRange on a copy of the generator state, and then the Firebird entry's NumRandomCalls draws are spent, which keeps the stream aligned with Mordor's own colour roll.
  • Result: the body type, the base model, the head record, the combat tree, and three piece slots set through the engine's slot setter 0x4C62EC (body, head, hair), with slot 7 cleared.

For a Berserker the body is the Buff torso for a scale up to 0.5 and the Stocky torso from 0.5; the Slim entry has no body row and is never chosen.

Console commands and other names

The console commands NemesisForge.CreateFollower (0x1C775EC) and NemesisForge.CreateNemesis (0x1C77604) create the stored follower or nemesis at once through 0x1C77514: if the payload is valid, 0xF636F0 makes the captain, then 0x1B5EE34 makes him his army's interrogation subject (army context +0x160) and fires the faction's SetInterrogationSubjectEvent. The quest path does not take that last step. The executable also names NemesisForge.Override.ClearAll, the configuration keys Events_NemesisForgeImportEnabled and Events_NemesisForgeImportSampled, the telemetry event name NemesisForgeImport, and a user property User.OwnsShadowOfMordor.
